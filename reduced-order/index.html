Reduced-order variational DA

Every variational method in this reference is a choice of control and prior: 3DVar estimates one state ((5.1)), strong-constraint 4DVar the initial state of a window ((6.1)), and weak-constraint 4DVar the initial state plus a model error after every step ((7.2)). Each of them has a reduced form, where we keep only a few coefficients of the correction (of the state, of the initial state, or of the model error) in a basis chosen beforehand. So the control drops from DxD_xDx​ or Dx(T+1)D_x (T + 1)Dx​(T+1) numbers to a few tens, the Hessian becomes small enough to factor, and the prior is far better conditioned. Incremental 4DVar ((8.3)) adds no new prior: it solves the strong-constraint pair a second way, by Gauss-Newton, and its reduced form does the same with the inner problem restricted to the basis.

This gives a ladder of eight methods, which we write in the same language as the GeoStack esmax ocean page Variational DA as programs.

The reduced control¶

Let Φ∈RDx×Dz\gmat{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_z}Φ∈RDx​×Dz​ be a basis matrix with Dz≪DxD_z \ll D_xDz​≪Dx​ columns and z∈RDz\vect{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z}z∈RDz​ its coefficients. The reduced control writes the state x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​ as the background mean μx∈RDx\gvect{\mu}_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}μx​∈RDx​ plus a correction in the span of the basis,

x=μx+Φz\vect{x} = \gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}x=μx​+Φz
(23.1)

with a Gaussian prior on the coefficients,

z∼N(0,Σz), so R(z)=12z⊤Σz−1z\vect{z} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \gmat{\Sigma}_z), \text{ so } \mathcal{R}(\vect{z}) = \frac{1}{2} \vect{z}^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} \vect{z}z∼N(0,Σz​), so R(z)=21​z⊤Σz−1​z
(23.2)

where Σz∈RDz×Dz\gmat{\Sigma}_z \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z \times D_z}Σz​∈RDz​×Dz​ is the prior covariance of the coefficients, which is diagonal for every built-in basis. The implied state covariance is ΦΣzΦ⊤\gmat{\Phi} \gmat{\Sigma}_z \gmat{\Phi}^\topΦΣz​Φ⊤, which has rank DzD_zDz​: the reduced methods simply cannot correct anything outside the span of Φ\gmat{\Phi}Φ.

A natural basis comes from Σx∈RDx×Dx\gmat{\Sigma}_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Σx​∈RDx​×Dx​ itself. If Σx=Udiag(σ2)U⊤\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \mat{U} \op{diag}(\gvect{\sigma}^2) \mat{U}^\topΣx​=Udiag(σ2)U⊤ is its eigendecomposition and UP∈RDx×Dz\mat{U}_P \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_z}UP​∈RDx​×Dz​, σP2∈RDz\gvect{\sigma}_P^2 \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z}σP2​∈RDz​ keep the DzD_zDz​ largest eigenpairs, then

Σx≈GG⊤, with G=UPdiag(σP)\gmat{\Sigma}_x \approx \mat{G} \mat{G}^\top, \text{ with } \mat{G} = \mat{U}_P \op{diag}(\gvect{\sigma}_P)Σx​≈GG⊤, with G=UP​diag(σP​)
(23.3)

and if we take Φ=G\gmat{\Phi} = \mat{G}Φ=G with Σz=I\gmat{\Sigma}_z = \mat{I}Σz​=I we get a whitened control: the coefficients are standard normal, the prior term is 12∥z∥22\frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{z} \Vert_2^221​∥z∥22​, and the minimiser no longer has to learn a Σx−1\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}Σx−1​ whose condition number can span several decades. These are the EOFs of the background error. A stationary Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ on a periodic grid is diagonalised by Fourier modes, so there G\mat{G}G is a truncated Fourier series, which is what the esmax page builds with kernellib features.

Every vardax basis carries its own Σz\gmat{\Sigma}_zΣz​ (API):

LinearParameterization(basis, background) turns a basis into a control for any model that takes parameterization=: it decodes z\vect{z}z with (23.1) and adds the penalty (23.2).

Running example¶

All the rungs below share one twin experiment: Lorenz-96 (15.2) on Dx=40D_x = 40Dx​=40 sites with forcing F=8F = 8F=8, one RK4 step of Δt=0.05\Delta t = 0.05Δt=0.05 per call, over a window of T=10T = 10T=10 steps (11 times). The background mean is μx=xtrue+Lξ\gvect{\mu}_x = \vect{x}_\text{true} + \mat{L} \gvect{\xi}μx​=xtrue​+Lξ with ξ∼N(0,I)\gvect{\xi} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mat{I})ξ∼N(0,I) and Σx=LL⊤∈RDx×Dx\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \mat{L} \mat{L}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Σx​=LL⊤∈RDx​×Dx​ a Gaussian correlation of length scale 2 sites plus a 10-3 jitter, and every second site is observed at every time with Σε=I\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = \mat{I}Σε​=I, so Dy=DxD_y = D_xDy​=Dx​. It is the example of chapter 6, and we rebuild it here so the page runs on its own.

It prints RMSE of μ_x 1.44.

The basis for every rung is (23.3) with Dz=8D_z = 8Dz​=8 and Dz=16D_z = 16Dz​=16.

It prints D_z = 8: 79% of tr Σ_x and D_z = 16: 99% of tr Σ_x. The block continues the one above.

Reduced 3DVar¶

Putting (23.1) into the 3DVar cost (5.1) gives a cost over DzD_zDz​ numbers, with y∈RDy\vect{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y∈RDy​, h:RDx→RDyh: \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_y}h:RDx​→RDy​ and Σε∈RDy×Dy\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σε​∈RDy​×Dy​,

J(z)=12z⊤Σz−1z+12∥y−h(μx+Φz)∥Σε−12\mathcal{J}(\vect{z}) = \frac{1}{2} \vect{z}^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} \vect{z} + \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{y} - h(\gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}}J(z)=21​z⊤Σz−1​z+21​∥y−h(μx​+Φz)∥Σε−1​2​
(23.4)

whose minimiser z⋆\vect{z}^\starz⋆ gives the analysis x⋆=μx+Φz⋆\vect{x}^\star = \gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}^\starx⋆=μx​+Φz⋆. In vardax this is ThreeDVar with parameterization=LinearParameterization(basis, background); the background term of (5.1) is replaced by the penalty, so prior_cov_op is not used.

It prints 3D-Var, full: RMSE 0.53, 3D-Var, D_z = 8: RMSE 0.57 and 3D-Var, D_z = 16: RMSE 0.52. With 99 % of the trace of Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ in 16 modes, the reduced analysis is as good as the full one; with 8 modes the correction misses the smaller scales.

Reduced strong-constraint 4DVar¶

The same substitution in the strong-constraint cost (6.1) gives

J(z)=12z⊤Σz−1z+12∑t=0T∥mt⊙(yt−ht(ft(μx+Φz)))∥Σε,t−12\mathcal{J}(\vect{z}) = \frac{1}{2} \vect{z}^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} \vect{z} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Vert \vect{m}_t \odot \left(\vect{y}_t - h_t(f^t(\gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}))\right) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t}^{-1}}J(z)=21​z⊤Σz−1​z+21​t=0∑T​∥mt​⊙(yt​−ht​(ft(μx​+Φz)))∥Σε,t−1​2​
(23.5)

where ftf^tft is the forward model fff applied ttt times, yt∈RDy\vect{y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}yt​∈RDy​, mt∈{0,1}Dy\vect{m}_t \in \{0, 1\}^{D_y}mt​∈{0,1}Dy​ is the observation mask at time ttt, hth_tht​ is the observation operator and Σε,t∈RDy×Dy\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σε,t​∈RDy​×Dy​ the observation-error covariance. It is minimised directly, with no linearisation, outer loop or inner CG. The gradient is the state-space adjoint projected onto the basis,

∇zJ=Σz−1z+Φ⊤x0†\nabla_{\vect{z}} \mathcal{J} = \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} \vect{z} + \gmat{\Phi}^\top \vect{x}^\dagger_0∇z​J=Σz−1​z+Φ⊤x0†​
(23.6)

where x0†∈RDx\vect{x}^\dagger_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x0†​∈RDx​ is the adjoint state at t=0t = 0t=0 from the backward sweep (6.5). One cost-and-gradient evaluation costs one forward and one adjoint run, as in state space. The gain is that the minimiser works in DzD_zDz​ dimensions, and when DzD_zDz​ is a few tens even forward-mode differentiation with DzD_zDz​ tangent runs and the full Dz×DzD_z \times D_zDz​×Dz​ Hessian become affordable. ReducedBasisFourDVar(basis=...) minimises (23.5); with basis=None it is the same as StrongFourDVar, and basis= is equivalent to StrongFourDVar(parameterization=LinearParameterization(basis, background)).

It prints strong 4D-Var, full: RMSE 0.26, strong 4D-Var, D_z = 8: RMSE 0.64 and strong 4D-Var, D_z = 16: RMSE 0.27. Every control uses the same Adam minimiser, so only the control differs. A chaotic forward can return non-finite values on an aggressive quasi-Newton trial step, and the masked Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​ solve raises on them; a first-order minimiser with a small step avoids it on this window. ReducedBasisFourDVar was called Incremental4DVar until vardax 0.1.10; the old name still works and emits a DeprecationWarning.

Reduced incremental 4DVar¶

Incremental 4DVar (8.3) linearises around the current iterate and solves a quadratic inner problem by CG, and with the control-variable transform (8.6) its unknowns are whitened. The reduced form keeps the same outer loop, which still runs the full nonlinear model, but restricts the increment to the basis, δx=Φδz\delta\vect{x} = \gmat{\Phi} \delta\vect{z}δx=Φδz. With the whitened basis (Σz=I\gmat{\Sigma}_z = \mat{I}Σz​=I) the inner cost at outer iterate z(i)\vect{z}^{(i)}z(i) is

δJ(δz)=12∥z(i)+δz∥22+12∑t=0T∥rt(i)−HtFtΦδz∥Σε,t−12\delta\mathcal{J}(\delta\vect{z}) = \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{z}^{(i)} + \delta\vect{z} \Vert_2^2 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Vert \vect{r}_t^{(i)} - \mat{H}_t \mat{F}_t \gmat{\Phi} \delta\vect{z} \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t}^{-1}}δJ(δz)=21​∥z(i)+δz∥22​+21​t=0∑T​∥rt(i)​−Ht​Ft​Φδz∥Σε,t−1​2​
(23.7)

where rt(i)=yt−ht(ft(μx+Φz(i)))∈RDy\vect{r}_t^{(i)} = \vect{y}_t - h_t(f^t(\gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}^{(i)})) \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}rt(i)​=yt​−ht​(ft(μx​+Φz(i)))∈RDy​ is the innovation of the current trajectory, and Ft∈RDx×Dx\mat{F}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Ft​∈RDx​×Dx​, Ht∈RDy×Dx\mat{H}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_x}Ht​∈RDy​×Dx​ are the tangent-linear operators of (8.1) and (8.2). Its normal equations are a Dz×DzD_z \times D_zDz​×Dz​ system,

(I+∑t=0T(HtFtΦ)⊤Σε,t−1(HtFtΦ))δz⋆=−z(i)+∑t=0T(HtFtΦ)⊤Σε,t−1rt(i)\left(\mat{I} + \sum_{t=0}^{T} (\mat{H}_t \mat{F}_t \gmat{\Phi})^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t}^{-1} (\mat{H}_t \mat{F}_t \gmat{\Phi})\right) \delta\vect{z}^\star = -\vect{z}^{(i)} + \sum_{t=0}^{T} (\mat{H}_t \mat{F}_t \gmat{\Phi})^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t}^{-1} \vect{r}_t^{(i)}(I+t=0∑T​(Ht​Ft​Φ)⊤Σε,t−1​(Ht​Ft​Φ))δz⋆=−z(i)+t=0∑T​(Ht​Ft​Φ)⊤Σε,t−1​rt(i)​
(23.8)

which is the CVT system (8.7) with the square root Σx1/2\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{1/2}Σx1/2​ replaced by the rectangular Φ\gmat{\Phi}Φ. Being Dz×DzD_z \times D_zDz​×Dz​, it is small enough to factor directly instead of iterating. The outer update is z(i+1)=z(i)+δz⋆\vect{z}^{(i+1)} = \vect{z}^{(i)} + \delta\vect{z}^\starz(i+1)=z(i)+δz⋆, and the analysis is x0⋆=μx+Φz⋆\vect{x}_0^\star = \gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}^\starx0⋆​=μx​+Φz⋆, the same minimiser as (23.5).

In vardax this is IncrementalFourDVar with a rectangular prior_sqrt_op: the control takes the input size of the square root, so passing Φ\gmat{\Phi}Φ in place of Σx1/2\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{1/2}Σx1/2​ makes the reduced form a strict generalisation of the CVT. ReducedBasisFourDVar minimises the same cost by quasi-Newton or a first-order minimiser instead.

It prints incremental 4D-Var, D_z = 16: RMSE 0.28: three Gauss-Newton outer loops over the same 16 coefficients land close to the first-order minimiser’s 0.27.

Reduced weak-constraint 4DVar¶

Weak-constraint 4DVar (7.2) adds a model error ηt∈RDx\gvect{\eta}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}ηt​∈RDx​ after every step, DxTD_x TDx​T more unknowns. If that error has a shape one can name, like a bias, a slow drift or a large-scale pattern, it is much better to estimate the few numbers of that shape. Let χ∈RT×C\gmat{\chi} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times C}χ∈RT×C be a temporal basis, for example Legendre polynomials over the window, ϕd\phi_dϕd​ the columns of a spatial basis Φ∈RDx×P\gmat{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times P}Φ∈RDx​×P, and W∈RC×P\mat{W} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times P}W∈RC×P the coefficients. The model error and its prior are

η(sn,t)=∑c=1C∑d=1Pχc(t)Wcdϕd(sn), with vecr(W)=(Σtime⊗Σspace)1/2ω, and ω∼N(0,I)\eta(s_n, t) = \sum_{c=1}^{C} \sum_{d=1}^{P} \chi_c(t) W_{cd} \phi_d(s_n), \text{ with } \op{vec}_r(\mat{W}) = (\gmat{\Sigma}_\text{time} \otimes \gmat{\Sigma}_\text{space})^{1/2} \gvect{\omega}, \text{ and } \gvect{\omega} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mat{I})η(sn​,t)=c=1∑C​d=1∑P​χc​(t)Wcd​ϕd​(sn​), with vecr​(W)=(Σtime​⊗Σspace​)1/2ω, and ω∼N(0,I)
(23.9)

where ω∈RCP\gvect{\omega} \in \mathbb{R}^{C P}ω∈RCP, vecr\op{vec}_rvecr​ stacks W\mat{W}W row by row and Σtime∈RC×C\gmat{\Sigma}_\text{time} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}Σtime​∈RC×C, Σspace∈RP×P\gmat{\Sigma}_\text{space} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times P}Σspace​∈RP×P are the diagonal prior variances of the temporal and spatial coefficients. The implied model-error covariance is Ση=(χ⊗Φ)(Σtime⊗Σspace)(χ⊗Φ)⊤\gmat{\Sigma}_\eta = (\gmat{\chi} \otimes \gmat{\Phi})(\gmat{\Sigma}_\text{time} \otimes \gmat{\Sigma}_\text{space})(\gmat{\chi} \otimes \gmat{\Phi})^\topΣη​=(χ⊗Φ)(Σtime​⊗Σspace​)(χ⊗Φ)⊤, never formed. The cost over the control (x0,ω)(\vect{x}_0, \gvect{\omega})(x0​,ω) is

J(x0,ω)=12∥x0−μx∥Σx−12+12∥ω∥22+12∑t=0T∥yt−ht(xt)∥Σε,t−12, with xt=f(xt−1)+ηt\mathcal{J}(\vect{x}_0, \gvect{\omega}) = \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{x}_0 - \gvect{\mu}_x \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}} + \frac{1}{2} \Vert \gvect{\omega} \Vert_2^2 + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Vert \vect{y}_t - h_t(\vect{x}_t) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t}^{-1}}, \text{ with } \vect{x}_t = f(\vect{x}_{t-1}) + \gvect{\eta}_tJ(x0​,ω)=21​∥x0​−μx​∥Σx−1​2​+21​∥ω∥22​+21​t=0∑T​∥yt​−ht​(xt​)∥Σε,t−1​2​, with xt​=f(xt−1​)+ηt​
(23.10)

so the control has Dx+CPD_x + C PDx​+CP numbers instead of Dx(T+1)D_x (T + 1)Dx​(T+1). A constant bias is the smallest case: C=1C = 1C=1 and P=1P = 1P=1, one coefficient. The initial state can be reduced as well, with (23.1) for x0\vect{x}_0x0​.

WeakFourDVar(parameterization=LinearParameterization(...)) reduces the initial state and model_err_basis= the model error. Below we first run the window with a biased model, F=7F = 7F=7 against the F=8F = 8F=8 truth, and we measure the error at the end of the window, where the bias has built up.

It prints strong 4D-Var, biased model: end-of-window RMSE 0.55, strong 4D-Var, exact model: end-of-window RMSE 0.42, weak 4D-Var, full x₀: end-of-window RMSE 0.53 and weak 4D-Var, D_z = 16 x₀: end-of-window RMSE 0.54. The weak constraint barely helps here, and changing σq\sigma_qσq​ to 0.05 or 0.32 gives 0.54 and 0.58: with 400 model-error unknowns against 220 observations, an error at every point and time cannot separate the bias from noise. A reduced model error with one constant mode is exactly that separation, and on a longer Lorenz-96 window the esmax page reports it recovering the bias as 1.00 ± 0.04 (truth 1.0). In vardax that is WeakFourDVar(model_err_basis=...), below.

It prints weak 4D-Var, one bias mode: end-of-window RMSE 0.43 and estimated bias per step 0.047. One coefficient brings the biased model to within 0.01 of the exact model’s 0.42, and the bias it finds is the forcing gap times the step, (8−7)×0.05=0.05(8 - 7) \times 0.05 = 0.05(8−7)×0.05=0.05, to first order.

Uncertainty¶

A reduced control has a reduced posterior: the Laplace precision lives in coefficient space, Dz×DzD_z \times D_zDz​×Dz​, and maps to a rank-DzD_zDz​ state covariance, (13.3) in chapter 13. The posterior adapters build it for any linear control, so posterior(batch) works for reduced 3DVar and reduced strong-constraint 4DVar. The spread outside the span of Φ\gmat{\Phi}Φ is exactly zero, which says more about the basis than about the data.

Choosing the basis and its size¶

  • EOFs of Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ ((23.3)) are the best rank-DzD_zDz​ approximation of the prior, so they are the default when Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ is known; the trace share ∥σP∥22/tr(Σx)\Vert \gvect{\sigma}_P \Vert_2^2 / \mathrm{tr}(\gmat{\Sigma}_x)∥σP​∥22​/tr(Σx​) says how much prior variance the control can still correct.

  • EOFs of a model run (eof_basis) adapt the control to the dynamics rather than to the prior, as in reduced-order incremental 4DVar for ocean models.

  • Fourier or RBF bases (fourier_basis, rbf_basis) suit a stationary or smooth correction and need no sample; wavelets (wavelet_basis) keep local features.

  • Size: we increase DzD_zDz​ until the analysis stops improving. On the running example the RMSE goes from 0.64 to 0.27 between Dz=8D_z = 8Dz​=8 and Dz=16D_z = 16Dz​=16 (79 % and 99 % of the trace), and the full control gives 0.26.
