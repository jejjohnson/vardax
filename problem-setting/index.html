Problem Setting

Data assimilation is the problem of estimating the state of a dynamical system given partial, noisy observations and prior knowledge. It sits at the intersection of Bayesian inference, optimisation and numerical analysis; the prior comes from a physical model (or a learned one), the likelihood comes from instruments, and the posterior is what we want.

The Bayesian picture¶

Let x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​ denote the state and y∈RDy\vect{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y∈RDy​ the observations. Bayes’ theorem gives the posterior

p(x∣y)∝p(y∣x)p(x)p(\vect{x} \mid \vect{y}) \propto p(\vect{y} \mid \vect{x}) p(\vect{x})p(x∣y)∝p(y∣x)p(x)
(1.1)

which has three ingredients:

  • Prior p(x)p(\vect{x})p(x). What we know about the state before seeing the data. Most commonly it is Gaussian, x∼N(μx,Σx)\vect{x} \sim \mathcal{N}(\gvect{\mu}_x, \gmat{\Sigma}_x)x∼N(μx​,Σx​) ((4.1)), with prior mean μx∈RDx\gvect{\mu}_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}μx​∈RDx​ and prior covariance Σx∈RDx×Dx\gmat{\Sigma}_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Σx​∈RDx​×Dx​. Data assimilation papers call them the background xb\vect{x}_bxb​ and the background-error covariance B\mat{B}B. In FourDVarNet the prior is replaced by a learned autoencoder; in AmortizedPosterior it is absorbed into the trained head.

  • Likelihood p(y∣x)p(\vect{y} \mid \vect{x})p(y∣x). How observations relate to the state. Chapter 2 models them as h(x)h(\vect{x})h(x) plus Gaussian noise with covariance Σε∈RDy×Dy\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σε​∈RDy​×Dy​ ((2.1)), so p(y∣x)=N(y;h(x),Σε)p(\vect{y} \mid \vect{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\vect{y}; h(\vect{x}), \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon)p(y∣x)=N(y;h(x),Σε​), where the observation operator h:RDx→RDyh : \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_y}h:RDx​→RDy​ maps state space to observation space. Its linear form or Jacobian is H∈RDy×Dx\mat{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_x}H∈RDy​×Dx​.

  • Posterior p(x∣y)p(\vect{x} \mid \vect{y})p(x∣y). What we infer about the state given the data. For a Gaussian prior and a Gaussian likelihood with linear hhh it is Gaussian in closed form ((4.3), chapter 4); otherwise we approximate it.

The maximum a posteriori (MAP) estimator minimises the negative log-posterior,

x⋆=argminx{−log⁡p(y∣x)−log⁡p(x)}\vect{x}^\star = \underset{\vect{x}}{\op{argmin}} \left\{-\log p(\vect{y} \mid \vect{x}) - \log p(\vect{x})\right\}x⋆=xargmin​{−logp(y∣x)−logp(x)}
(1.2)

with x⋆∈RDx\vect{x}^\star \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x⋆∈RDx​. With the Gaussian prior and likelihood above the bracket is, up to a constant, the 3DVar cost J(x)\mathcal{J}(\vect{x})J(x) of (5.1), chapter 5. When the state evolves through dynamics fff, we add a time index and a forward rollout, which gives 4DVar (chapters 6 to 8).

When dynamics enter: the 4D problem¶

Consider a state x0\vect{x}_0x0​ at the start of an assimilation window and observations yt\vect{y}_tyt​ at times t=0,1,…,Tt = 0, 1, \ldots, Tt=0,1,…,T, so the window has TTT steps and T+1T + 1T+1 times. A dynamical model f:RDx→RDxf : \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_x}f:RDx​→RDx​ propagates x0\vect{x}_0x0​ forward, and its ttt-fold composition ftf_tft​ gives

xt=ft(x0)\vect{x}_t = f_t(\vect{x}_0)xt​=ft​(x0​)
(1.3)

where xt∈RDx\vect{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}xt​∈RDx​ is the state at time ttt and yt∈RDy\vect{y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}yt​∈RDy​ the observations at time ttt.

The 4D variational cost adds an observation term for every time to the background term. In strong-constraint 4DVar (chapter 6) the dynamics are exact, the control variable is x0\vect{x}_0x0​ alone and the cost is (6.1). In weak-constraint 4DVar (chapter 7) each step carries a model error ηt∈RDx\gvect{\eta}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}ηt​∈RDx​ with covariance Ση,t∈RDx×Dx\gmat{\Sigma}_{\eta,t} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Ση,t​∈RDx​×Dx​, the cost (7.2) gains a model-error term, and the control becomes the augmented vector (x0,η1,…,ηT)(\vect{x}_0, \gvect{\eta}_1, \ldots, \gvect{\eta}_T)(x0​,η1​,…,ηT​).

In the linear-Gaussian limit (linear hth_tht​, linear ftf_tft​, Gaussian Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​, Σε,t\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,t}Σε,t​, Ση,t\gmat{\Sigma}_{\eta,t}Ση,t​) the posterior is Gaussian in closed form. In all other cases we minimise J\mathcal{J}J iteratively. The cost itself is the same; the solver is where the methods diverge.

They are siblings, not subclasses, and each one is appropriate to a different regime.

The role of the forward model¶

The dynamics fff and the observation operator hth_tht​ are the physics. vardax does not own them; somax (geophysics), plumax (atmospheric transport and methane) or any user code can supply them, as long as they satisfy pipekit_cycle.ForwardModel and pipekit_cycle.ObservationOperator respectively.

This means the same analysis class accepts any forward. A StrongFourDVar doesn’t know whether fff is a shallow-water solver from somax, a Lagrangian particle simulator from plumax, or a neural emulator trained from physics outputs. The interface is fixed; the implementation is a slot.

This separation is what makes vardax composable with the broader JAX ecosystem (diffrax, optimistix, lineax, gaussx, pipekit-cycle) without bespoke per-method wiring.

Computational ingredients¶

The four computations that recur in every DA method:

  1. Forward model rollout (1.3). ODE integration via diffrax; vardax composes it but does not implement it.

  2. Tangent-linear and adjoint Ft\mat{F}_tFt​, Ft⊤\mat{F}_t^\topFt⊤​, the Jacobian of ftf_tft​ and its transpose. Computed via diffrax.AbstractAdjoint (recursive checkpointing, continuous backsolve or forward sensitivity; see chapter 12).

  3. Observation operator ht(xt)h_t(\vect{x}_t)ht​(xt​) and its tangent linear Ht\mat{H}_tHt​. See chapter 2 (linearize() contract via lineax).

  4. Inner minimisation argminxJ(x)\op{argmin}_{\vect{x}} \mathcal{J}(\vect{x})argminx​J(x). Via optimistix.AbstractMinimiser (Gauss-Newton, BFGS, NonlinearCG, fixed-point iteration). For incremental 4DVar, argmin\op{argmin}argmin is done by CG on the linearised quadratic subproblem.

Each computation is owned by an upstream library. vardax composes them into DA algorithms; it does not reimplement them.

Posterior, not point estimate¶

A MAP x⋆\vect{x}^\starx⋆ is the start of the answer, not the end. Real DA produces a posterior (mean, covariance and provenance) that downstream consumers (population models, alert systems, decision pipelines) need.

vardax exposes a Posterior(mean, cov, samples, provenance) container returned by every analysis. Approximations include the Laplace (LaplaceCovariance) at MAP, the Gauss-Newton Hessian inversion (GaussNewtonHessian, free for IncrementalFourDVar), the ensemble covariance (EnsembleCovariance via filterax), or direct sampling from amortized heads. See chapter 13.

For the closed-form case (OptimalInterpolation) the posterior is exact, with the covariance (4.5) derived in chapter 4.

Reading order¶

Chapters 1 to 3 establish the shared foundation: this chapter (the problem), chapter 2 (the observation model) and chapter 3 (the dynamical model and its adjoint).

Chapters 4 to 10 cover one analysis method each, in the order of the table above, from OptimalInterpolation in chapter 4 to AmortizedPosterior in chapter 10, so in increasing order of sophistication.

Chapters 11 to 14 cover cross-cutting concerns: observation operators (the averaging-kernel and multi-instrument family), adjoint composition via optimistix and diffrax, posterior covariance, and the six-step research-to-operations methodology.

Chapters 15 to 17 are end-to-end examples on Lorenz, SSH and methane.

The companion design docs in design/ document the API contracts and architectural decisions behind these methods.
