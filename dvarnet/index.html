4DVarNet (learned 4DVar)

4DVarNet replaces two pieces of classical 4DVar with learned counterparts: the Gaussian prior ∥x−xb∥B−12\|x - x_b\|^2_{B^{-1}}∥x−xb​∥B−12​ becomes a learned reconstruction ∥x−φθ(x)∥2\|x - \varphi_\theta(x)\|^2∥x−φθ​(x)∥2, and the inner gradient-descent solver becomes a learned iteration with a ConvLSTM gradient modulator. The result is trainable end-to-end on (input, target) pairs.

The “Net” naming follows Fablet et al. (2021). vardax keeps it as the name of the method, not the family: 4DVarNet is one of seven peer analysis methods (Decision D14), a learned variant of strong-constraint 4DVar distinguished by its learned components, and not the parent of classical 4DVar.

Cost function¶

The variational cost is structurally familiar:

J(x)=αobs12∥H(x)−y∥R−12+αprior∥x−φθ(x)∥2J(x) = \alpha_\text{obs} \frac{1}{2} \|H(x) - y\|^2_{R^{-1}} + \alpha_\text{prior} \|x - \varphi_\theta(x)\|^2J(x)=αobs​21​∥H(x)−y∥R−12​+αprior​∥x−φθ​(x)∥2
(9.1)

where the first term is the observation cost (2.2), φθ\varphi_\thetaφθ​ is a learned autoencoder with parameters θ\thetaθ, and αobs\alpha_\text{obs}αobs​ and αprior\alpha_\text{prior}αprior​ weight the two terms. The Gaussian background term ∥x−xb∥B−12\|x - x_b\|^2_{B^{-1}}∥x−xb​∥B−12​ is replaced (not added). The learned prior is expressive enough to encode the same information that BBB + xbx_bxb​ would, plus structure that classical Gaussian priors can’t represent (multimodality, nonlinear manifolds, conditional dependencies).

The learned inner solver¶

Classical 4DVar minimises JJJ via gradient descent or a quasi-Newton method. 4DVarNet replaces the inner step with a learned modulator:

xk+1=xk−Φϕ(∇xJ(xk),hk), for k=0,…,K−1x_{k+1} = x_k - \Phi_\phi(\nabla_x J(x_k), h_k), \text{ for } k = 0, \ldots, K-1xk+1​=xk​−Φϕ​(∇x​J(xk​),hk​), for k=0,…,K−1
(9.2)

where JJJ is the cost (9.1), Φϕ\Phi_\phiΦϕ​ is a small neural network (ConvLSTM, MLP, or attention) with parameters ϕ\phiϕ, and hkh_khk​ is its recurrent hidden state. The network sees the current gradient and decides how to step, so it can learn momentum, adaptive step sizes, or problem-specific preconditioning that hand-coded solvers don’t capture.

After KKK iterations the analysis is x∗=xKx^* = x_Kx∗=xK​. The whole pipeline (forward, gradient, modulator, update) is differentiable end-to-end.

Training¶

The training objective minimises reconstruction error against ground-truth states:

L(θ,ϕ)=E(y,xtrue)∥x∗(θ,ϕ;y)−xtrue∥2\mathcal{L}(\theta, \phi) = \mathbb{E}_{(y, x_\text{true})} \|x^*(\theta, \phi; y) - x_\text{true}\|^2L(θ,ϕ)=E(y,xtrue​)​∥x∗(θ,ϕ;y)−xtrue​∥2
(9.3)

where x∗(θ,ϕ;y)x^*(\theta, \phi; y)x∗(θ,ϕ;y) is the analysis after KKK steps of (9.2) from the observations yyy, and xtruex_\text{true}xtrue​ is the ground-truth state. The training gradient ∇θ,ϕL\nabla_{\theta, \phi} \mathcal{L}∇θ,ϕ​L flows through the inner solver. This is where the adjoint choice (Decision D15) matters.

Adjoint choices for the inner solver¶

Vardax exposes solver_adjoint: optimistix.AbstractAdjoint as a constructor slot. Three viable choices:

AdjointMemoryConvergence requirementNotes
optimistix.RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint()O(K)O(K)O(K) checkpointsNoneStandard backprop with recursive checkpointing
vardax.adjoints.OneStepAdjoint()O(1)O(1)O(1)NoneBolte et al. 2023; only the last step differentiable
optimistix.ImplicitAdjoint()O(1)O(1)O(1)YesIFT-based at the fixed point; exact at convergence

RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint is the default. It backpropagates through all KKK unrolled steps, with recursive checkpointing to bound memory, so it always works and costs O(K)O(K)O(K) checkpoints.

OneStepAdjoint runs K−1K-1K−1 steps with jax.lax.stop_gradient applied to the trajectory, then a single differentiable step, so the training gradient picks up only the last iteration’s contribution. Bolte et al. (2023) prove this is exact at convergence; in practice it works well for converged or near-converged inner solvers. Its memory cost is O(1)O(1)O(1), which makes it the right choice for K≥20K \ge 20K≥20 when memory is constrained.

vardax ships OneStepAdjoint in vardax._src.adjoints.one_step with the goal of upstreaming to optimistix once stable (Decision D6).

ImplicitAdjoint uses the implicit function theorem at the fixed point x∗=f(x∗;θ)x^* = f(x^*; \theta)x∗=f(x∗;θ) of the inner iteration:

dx∗dθ=(I−∂f∂x)−1∂f∂θ\frac{d x^*}{d \theta} = \left(I - \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)^{-1} \frac{\partial f}{\partial \theta}dθdx∗​=(I−∂x∂f​)−1∂θ∂f​
(9.4)

where fff is the inner-step map (9.2) and both partial derivatives are taken at x∗x^*x∗. It is the implicit gradient (12.2) of chapter 12 with the stationarity condition replaced by the fixed-point residual x−f(x;θ)=0x - f(x; \theta) = 0x−f(x;θ)=0. Memory O(1)O(1)O(1), gradient exact at convergence. Requires the inner solver to actually converge; if KKK is too small, the IFT gradient is wrong. Use when the inner iteration is known to be a contraction.

When to use FourDVarNet¶

Use FourDVarNet when:

  • You have a training set of (observation, ground-truth) pairs

  • The classical BBB is hard to specify or known to be inadequate

  • The regime is data-rich and learning-friendly (smooth manifolds, no catastrophic distribution shift between train and test)

Don’t use FourDVarNet when:

  • You have no training pairs; go with classical methods (chapters 4 to 8)

  • The training distribution doesn’t match deployment, since FourDVarNet extrapolates poorly outside its training manifold

  • You need defensible posterior covariance, since the Laplace approximation (13.1) around a learned-prior MAP can be miscalibrated; IncrementalFourDVar with a classical Matérn prior gives a more honest posterior

Posterior¶

LaplaceCovariance builds its Hessian from the model’s obs_op, BBB and RRR, which FourDVarNet2D does not have, so it raises AttributeError on this model. A note on history¶

4DVarNet originated as a research line (Fablet et al. 2021, 2023) that demonstrated end-to-end learning of variational DA on satellite SSH. The original codebase (CIA-Oceanix/4dvarnet-starter) is PyTorch and ocean-specific. Vardax brings the same algorithm into JAX, peers it with the classical DA hierarchy, and connects it to the broader ecosystem (somax / plumax for physics, gaussx for structured priors, filterax for hybrid EnVar, pipekit-cycle for orchestration).

In the v0.4 design the classical methods are the foundation, not legacy, and 4DVarNet earns its place beside them as a useful learned variant.
