4DVarNet (learned 4DVar)

4DVarNet replaces two pieces of classical 4DVar with learned counterparts: the Gaussian prior ∥x−μx∥Σx−12\Vert \vect{x} - \gvect{\mu}_x \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}}∥x−μx​∥Σx−1​2​ becomes a learned reconstruction ∥x−gae(x;θ)∥22\Vert \vect{x} - g_\text{ae}(\vect{x}; \gvect{\theta}) \Vert_2^2∥x−gae​(x;θ)∥22​, and the inner gradient-descent solver becomes a learned iteration with a ConvLSTM gradient modulator. The result is trainable end-to-end on (input, target) pairs.

The “Net” naming follows Fablet et al. (2021). vardax keeps it as the name of the method, not the family: 4DVarNet is one of seven peer analysis methods (Decision D14), a learned variant of strong-constraint 4DVar distinguished by its learned components, and not the parent of classical 4DVar.

Cost function¶

The variational cost is structurally familiar:

J(x)=αo12∥h(x)−y∥Σε−12+αp∥x−gae(x;θ)∥22\mathcal{J}(\vect{x}) = \alpha_o \frac{1}{2} \Vert h(\vect{x}) - \vect{y} \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}} + \alpha_p \Vert \vect{x} - g_\text{ae}(\vect{x}; \gvect{\theta}) \Vert_2^2J(x)=αo​21​∥h(x)−y∥Σε−1​2​+αp​∥x−gae​(x;θ)∥22​
(9.1)

where the first term is the observation cost (2.2), gaeg_\text{ae}gae​ is a learned autoencoder with parameters θ\gvect{\theta}θ, and αo\alpha_oαo​ and αp\alpha_pαp​ weight the two terms. Here x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​ is the state of a window and y∈RDy\vect{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y∈RDy​ its observations, h:RDx→RDyh: \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_y}h:RDx​→RDy​, Σε∈RDy×Dy\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σε​∈RDy​×Dy​, gae:RDx→RDxg_\text{ae}: \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_x}gae​:RDx​→RDx​ and θ∈RDθ\gvect{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_\theta}θ∈RDθ​. The Gaussian background term ∥x−μx∥Σx−12\Vert \vect{x} - \gvect{\mu}_x \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}}∥x−μx​∥Σx−1​2​ is replaced (not added). The learned prior is expressive enough to encode the same information that Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ + μx\gvect{\mu}_xμx​ would, plus structure that classical Gaussian priors can’t represent (multimodality, nonlinear manifolds, conditional dependencies).

The learned inner solver¶

Classical 4DVar minimises J\mathcal{J}J via gradient descent or a quasi-Newton method. 4DVarNet replaces the inner step with a learned modulator:

x(i+1)=x(i)−αΔx(i), with (Δx(i),c(i+1))=gmod(∇xJ(x(i)),x(i),c(i)), for i=0,…,Niter−1\vect{x}^{(i+1)} = \vect{x}^{(i)} - \alpha \Delta\vect{x}^{(i)}, \text{ with } (\Delta\vect{x}^{(i)}, \vect{c}^{(i+1)}) = g_\text{mod}(\nabla_{\vect{x}} \mathcal{J}(\vect{x}^{(i)}), \vect{x}^{(i)}, \vect{c}^{(i)}), \text{ for } i = 0, \ldots, N_\text{iter} - 1x(i+1)=x(i)−αΔx(i), with (Δx(i),c(i+1))=gmod​(∇x​J(x(i)),x(i),c(i)), for i=0,…,Niter​−1
(9.2)

where J\mathcal{J}J is the cost (9.1), gmodg_\text{mod}gmod​ is a small neural network (ConvLSTM, MLP, or attention) with parameters θmod\gvect{\theta}_\text{mod}θmod​, c(i)\vect{c}^{(i)}c(i) is its recurrent carry, α>0\alpha > 0α>0 is the step size on the state, and NiterN_\text{iter}Niter​ is the number of solver steps. The network sees the current gradient and decides how to step, so it can learn momentum, adaptive step sizes, or problem-specific preconditioning that hand-coded solvers don’t capture. Here Δx(i)∈RDx\Delta\vect{x}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}Δx(i)∈RDx​, and the carry c(i)\vect{c}^{(i)}c(i) holds a hidden and a cell tensor with hidden_dim channels per cell.

After NiterN_\text{iter}Niter​ iterations the analysis is x⋆=x(Niter)\vect{x}^\star = \vect{x}^{(N_\text{iter})}x⋆=x(Niter​). The whole pipeline (forward, gradient, modulator, update) is differentiable end-to-end.

Training¶

The training objective minimises reconstruction error against ground-truth states:

L(θ,θmod)=E(y,xtrue)∥x⋆(θ,θmod;y)−xtrue∥22\mathcal{L}(\gvect{\theta}, \gvect{\theta}_\text{mod}) = \mathbb{E}_{(\vect{y}, \vect{x}_\text{true})} \Vert \vect{x}^\star(\gvect{\theta}, \gvect{\theta}_\text{mod}; \vect{y}) - \vect{x}_\text{true} \Vert_2^2L(θ,θmod​)=E(y,xtrue​)​∥x⋆(θ,θmod​;y)−xtrue​∥22​
(9.3)

where x⋆(θ,θmod;y)\vect{x}^\star(\gvect{\theta}, \gvect{\theta}_\text{mod}; \vect{y})x⋆(θ,θmod​;y) is the analysis after NiterN_\text{iter}Niter​ steps of (9.2) from the observations y\vect{y}y, and xtrue∈RDx\vect{x}_\text{true} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}xtrue​∈RDx​ is the ground-truth state. The training gradient ∇θ,θmodL\nabla_{\gvect{\theta}, \gvect{\theta}_\text{mod}} \mathcal{L}∇θ,θmod​​L flows through the inner solver. This is where the adjoint choice (Decision D15) matters.

Adjoint choices for the inner solver¶

Vardax exposes solver_adjoint: optimistix.AbstractAdjoint as a constructor slot. Three viable choices:

AdjointMemoryConvergence requirementNotes
optimistix.RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint()O(Niter)O(N_\text{iter})O(Niter​) checkpointsNoneStandard backprop with recursive checkpointing
vardax.adjoints.OneStepAdjoint()O(1)O(1)O(1)NoneBolte et al. 2023; only the last step differentiable
optimistix.ImplicitAdjoint()O(1)O(1)O(1)YesIFT-based at the fixed point; exact at convergence

RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint is the default. It backpropagates through all NiterN_\text{iter}Niter​ unrolled steps, with recursive checkpointing to bound memory, so it always works and costs O(Niter)O(N_\text{iter})O(Niter​) checkpoints.

OneStepAdjoint runs Niter−1N_\text{iter} - 1Niter​−1 steps with jax.lax.stop_gradient applied to the trajectory, then a single differentiable step, so the training gradient picks up only the last iteration’s contribution. Bolte et al. (2023) prove this is exact at convergence; in practice it works well for converged or near-converged inner solvers. Its memory cost is O(1)O(1)O(1), which makes it the right choice for Niter≥20N_\text{iter} \ge 20Niter​≥20 when memory is constrained.

vardax ships OneStepAdjoint in vardax._src.adjoints.one_step with the goal of upstreaming to optimistix once stable (Decision D6).

ImplicitAdjoint uses the implicit function theorem at the fixed point x⋆=gstep(x⋆;θ)\vect{x}^\star = g_\text{step}(\vect{x}^\star; \gvect{\theta})x⋆=gstep​(x⋆;θ) of the inner iteration:

(9.4)

where gstep:RDx→RDxg_\text{step}: \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_x}gstep​:RDx​→RDx​ is the inner-step map (9.2), θ\gvect{\theta}θ stands for either set of weights, and both partial derivatives are taken at x⋆\vect{x}^\starx⋆. It is the implicit gradient (12.2) of chapter 12 with the stationarity condition replaced by the fixed-point residual x−gstep(x;θ)=0\vect{x} - g_\text{step}(\vect{x}; \gvect{\theta}) = \mathbf{0}x−gstep​(x;θ)=0. Memory O(1)O(1)O(1), gradient exact at convergence. Requires the inner solver to actually converge; if NiterN_\text{iter}Niter​ is too small, the IFT gradient is wrong. When to use FourDVarNet¶

Use FourDVarNet when:

  • You have a training set of (observation, ground-truth) pairs

  • The classical Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ is hard to specify or known to be inadequate

  • The regime is data-rich and learning-friendly (smooth manifolds, no catastrophic distribution shift between train and test)

Don’t use FourDVarNet when:

  • You have no training pairs; go with classical methods (chapters 4 to 8)

  • The training distribution doesn’t match deployment, since FourDVarNet extrapolates poorly outside its training manifold

  • You need defensible posterior covariance, since the Laplace approximation (13.1) around a learned-prior MAP can be miscalibrated; IncrementalFourDVar with a classical Matérn prior gives a more honest posterior

A note on history¶

4DVarNet originated as a research line (Fablet et al. 2021, 2023) that demonstrated end-to-end learning of variational DA on satellite SSH. The original codebase (CIA-Oceanix/4dvarnet-starter) is PyTorch and ocean-specific. Vardax brings the same algorithm into JAX, peers it with the classical DA hierarchy, and connects it to the broader ecosystem (somax / plumax for physics, gaussx for structured priors, filterax for hybrid EnVar, pipekit-cycle for orchestration).

In the v0.4 design the classical methods are the foundation, not legacy, and 4DVarNet earns its place beside them as a useful learned variant.
