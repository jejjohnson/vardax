Incremental 4DVar with Control-Variable Transform

Operational 4DVar at ECMWF, NCEP, JMA, and UKMO all follow a common template: Gauss-Newton outer iterations on the full nonlinear cost, CG (or Lanczos) inner iterations on a linearised quadratic subproblem, with a control-variable transform that preconditions the inner solve. IncrementalFourDVar packages this pattern.

It solves the same problem as StrongFourDVar (chapter 6) and gives the same answer, but the specialised inner solver is much faster when the prior is structured (Matérn, Kronecker) and the window is long.

On later loops it does not vanish: dropping it would pull every increment toward x(i)\vect{x}^{(i)}x(i) instead of μx\gvect{\mu}_xμx​, and the iteration would no longer converge to the 4DVar minimum.

This pattern converges in a handful of outer iterations (typically 3 to 5) regardless of the strength of the nonlinearity, because each outer step sees a fresh tangent linearisation.

Control-variable transform (CVT)¶

The Gauss-Newton Hessian inherits the condition number of Σx−1\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}Σx−1​: a smooth (long-correlation) Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ has tiny eigenvalues, which become huge ones of Σx−1\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}Σx−1​. In state space every Hessian product also needs Σx−1v\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1} \vect{v}Σx−1​v, which IncrementalFourDVar would have to compute by a nested CG solve.

The CVT changes variables. Let Lx∈RDx×Dx\mat{L}_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Lx​∈RDx​×Dx​ be a square root of Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​, LxLx⊤=Σx\mat{L}_x \mat{L}_x^\top = \gmat{\Sigma}_xLx​Lx⊤​=Σx​, and write the initial state through the whitened control z∈RDx\vect{z} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}z∈RDx​ as

x0=μx+Lxz\vect{x}_0 = \gvect{\mu}_x + \mat{L}_x \vect{z}x0​=μx​+Lx​z
(8.6)

IncrementalConfig(cvt=True) (the default) uses it. Lx\mat{L}_xLx​ is prior_sqrt_op= when given (a Cholesky factor, a structured operator’s own square root, or gaussx.sqrt(prior_cov_op, lanczos_order=k) for a large unstructured Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​), else gaussx.sqrt(prior_cov_op): closed form for diagonal, Kronecker and block-diagonal Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​, a dense eigendecomposition otherwise. cvt=False keeps the state-space solve, with the background residual whitened by the Cholesky factor of Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​.

Implementation in vardax¶

The example below is a Y×X=4×5Y \times X = 4 \times 5Y×X=4×5 grid (Dx=20D_x = 20Dx​=20 cells) over a window of T=3T = 3T=3 steps, which holds T+1=4T + 1 = 4T+1=4 times, with a nonlinear forward f(x)=x+0.2sin⁡(x)f(\vect{x}) = \vect{x} + 0.2 \sin(\vect{x})f(x)=x+0.2sin(x) applied cell by cell. Σx=Σrow⊗Σcol\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \gmat{\Sigma}_\text{row} \otimes \gmat{\Sigma}_\text{col}Σx​=Σrow​⊗Σcol​ is separable, the factors Σrow∈RY×Y\gmat{\Sigma}_\text{row} \in \mathbb{R}^{Y \times Y}Σrow​∈RY×Y and Σcol∈RX×X\gmat{\Sigma}_\text{col} \in \mathbb{R}^{X \times X}Σcol​∈RX×X each a Gaussian correlation of length scale 1.5 cells (plus a 10-1 jitter), and the background is μx=0\gvect{\mu}_x = \mathbf{0}μx​=0. Every cell is observed through an averaging kernel (2.5) with Jx^=I\mat{J}_{\hat{x}} = \mat{I}Jx^​=I, w=0.8⋅1\vect{w} = 0.8 \cdot \mathbf{1}w=0.8⋅1 and μret=0\gvect{\mu}_\text{ret} = \mathbf{0}μret​=0, so Dy=DxD_y = D_xDy​=Dx​, and Σε=0.04I\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = 0.04 \mat{I}Σε​=0.04I.

It prints largest gap to StrongFourDVar 2.0e-03: three outer loops of at most 20 CG steps each land that close to the BFGS minimum of the same cost.

The model solves the window as whitened residuals with optimistix.least_squares, and takes its budget as arguments:

  • solver: the least-squares solver, by default argminax.solve.GaussNewtonCG(rtol=0, atol=0, max_cg_steps=30, cg_rtol=1e-6, cg_atol=1e-6). Its outer tolerances of 0 run exactly n_outer outer iterations, and each inner CG stops at its tolerance or after max_cg_steps steps (truncated CG). Positive rtol and atol stop the outer loop once it has converged.

  • n_outer: the most Gauss-Newton outer iterations (default 3).

IncrementalConfig holds the switches:

  • cvt: solve over z\vect{z}z of (8.6) (default True).

  • covariance_tol: tolerance of the default posterior’s CG solves; Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​ and Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ enter the analysis through their Cholesky factors.

  • jac: "fwd" (default) applies HtFt←0\mat{H}_t \mat{F}_{t \leftarrow 0}Ht​Ft←0​ by jax.linearize, matrix-free. "bwd" forms the window’s Jacobian with jax.jacrev, one VJP per observation, and runs CG on that matrix. Pass it when the forward or observation map defines only a reverse-mode rule, such as a jax.custom_vjp or a diffrax solve under RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint; it suits windows of up to a few thousand observations, and the posterior uses the same mode.

Its n_outer, n_inner, cg_atol and cg_rtol fields still build the equivalent GaussNewtonCG for one release, with a DeprecationWarning.

A static control, such as a source field rather than an initial state, is an identity-step forward (step returns its input) with a one-time window: the outer loop is then Gauss-Newton 3D-Var through any obs_op.

Why it’s the operational fast path¶

Three multiplicative speedups over StrongFourDVar with NonlinearCG:

  1. CVT preconditioning. CG converges in O(10)O(10)O(10) iterations instead of O(κ(Σx))O(\sqrt{\kappa(\gmat{\Sigma}_x)})O(κ(Σx​)​), where κ(Σx)\kappa(\gmat{\Sigma}_x)κ(Σx​) is the prior condition number.

  2. Linearisation reuse. The tangent linear Ft←0\mat{F}_{t \leftarrow 0}Ft←0​ is computed once per outer iteration and reused across all n_inner inner CG steps, whereas NonlinearCG on the full nonlinear cost recomputes the gradient (a full forward and adjoint pass) at every step.

  3. Cheap posterior. The posterior precision is the operator the inner loop solves with (see the posterior section below).

For a typical operational problem (a 12-hour window, Dx=107D_x = 10^7Dx​=107 grid points, Dy=105D_y = 10^5Dy​=105 observations), IncrementalFourDVar converges in minutes; StrongFourDVar with NonlinearCG takes hours.

Strong- vs weak-constraint¶

IncrementalFourDVar is strong-constraint by default. For weak-constraint incremental 4DVar (the augmented control vector with ηt\gvect{\eta}_tηt​), use WeakFourDVar directly, since the incremental machinery for the augmented problem is heavier and not always worth the complexity. The strong / weak choice is structural; the inner-solver choice (general vs incremental) is performance.

When to switch from IncrementalFourDVar to StrongFourDVar¶

Almost never, for operational work. IncrementalFourDVar is faster and produces the same answer. Use StrongFourDVar only when:

  • Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ is large, unstructured and has no cheap square root, so gaussx.sqrt would fall back to a dense eigendecomposition. Pass a Lanczos square root (prior_sqrt_op=gaussx.sqrt(prior_cov_op, lanczos_order=k)) or use cvt=False before giving up on the incremental solver.

  • You want to swap in a custom optimistix minimiser (e.g. optx.LevenbergMarquardt for very nonlinear cases). Incremental uses a fixed GN+CG; the rest of the optimistix zoo isn’t compatible with the incremental machinery.

For most users, IncrementalFourDVar is the right default.
