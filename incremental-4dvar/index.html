Incremental 4DVar with Control-Variable Transform

Operational 4DVar at ECMWF, NCEP, JMA, and UKMO all follow a common template: Gauss-Newton outer iterations on the full nonlinear cost, CG (or Lanczos) inner iterations on a linearised quadratic subproblem, with a control-variable transform that preconditions the inner solve. IncrementalFourDVar packages this pattern.

It solves the same problem as StrongFourDVar (chapter 6) and gives the same answer, but the specialised inner solver is much faster when the prior is structured (Matérn, Kronecker) and the window is long.

This pattern converges in a handful of outer iterations (typically 3 to 5) regardless of the strength of the nonlinearity, because each outer step sees a fresh tangent linearisation.

Control-variable transform (CVT)¶

The Hessian HGN\mathbf{H}_\text{GN}HGN​ inherits the condition number of B−1B^{-1}B−1: a smooth (long-correlation) BBB has tiny eigenvalues, which become huge ones of B−1B^{-1}B−1. In state space every Hessian product also needs B−1vB^{-1}vB−1v, which IncrementalFourDVar would have to compute by a nested CG solve.

The CVT changes variables. Let SSS be a square root of BBB, SS⊤=BS S^\top = BSS⊤=B, and write the departure from the background as

IncrementalConfig(cvt=True) (the default) uses it. SSS is prior_sqrt_op= when given (a Cholesky factor, a structured operator’s own square root, or gaussx.sqrt(B, lanczos_order=k) for a large unstructured BBB), else gaussx.sqrt(prior_cov_op): closed form for diagonal, Kronecker and block-diagonal BBB, a dense eigendecomposition otherwise. cvt=False keeps the state-space solve, with B−1B^{-1}B−1 and R−1R^{-1}R−1 applied by CG at tolerance covariance_tol.

IncrementalConfig:

  • n_outer: number of Gauss-Newton outer iterations (typically 3).

  • n_inner: maximum CG iterations per outer iteration (typically 20 to 50); the inner solve is truncated there.

  • cg_atol, cg_rtol: inner CG convergence tolerances.

  • cvt: solve over χ\chiχ of (8.6) (default True).

  • covariance_tol: tolerance of the CG solves that apply R−1R^{-1}R−1 (and B−1B^{-1}B−1 when cvt=False).

  • jac: "fwd" (default) applies Ht′Mt′H'_t M'_tHt′​Mt′​ by jax.linearize, matrix-free. "bwd" forms the window’s Jacobian with jax.jacrev, one VJP per observation, and runs CG on that matrix. Pass it when the forward or observation map defines only a reverse-mode rule, such as a jax.custom_vjp or a diffrax solve under RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint; it suits windows of up to a few thousand observations, and the posterior uses the same mode.

A static control, such as a source field rather than an initial state, is an identity-step forward (step returns its input) with a one-time window: the outer loop is then Gauss-Newton 3D-Var through any obs_op.

Why it’s the operational fast path¶

Three multiplicative speedups over StrongFourDVar with NonlinearCG:

  1. CVT preconditioning. CG converges in O(10)O(10)O(10) iterations instead of O(κ(B))O(\sqrt{\kappa(B)})O(κ(B)​), where κ(B)\kappa(B)κ(B) is the prior condition number.

  2. Linearisation reuse. The tangent linear M′M'M′ is computed once per outer iteration and reused across all ninnern_\text{inner}ninner​ inner CG steps, whereas NonlinearCG on the full nonlinear cost recomputes the gradient (a full forward and adjoint pass) at every step.

  3. Cheap posterior. The posterior precision is the operator the inner loop solves with (see the posterior section below).

For a typical operational problem (T=12T = 12T=12 hours, n=107n = 10^7n=107 grid points, m=105m = 10^5m=105 observations), IncrementalFourDVar converges in minutes; StrongFourDVar with NonlinearCG takes hours.

Strong- vs weak-constraint¶

IncrementalFourDVar is strong-constraint by default. For weak-constraint incremental 4DVar (the augmented control vector with ηt\eta_tηt​), use WeakFourDVar directly, since the incremental machinery for the augmented problem is heavier and not always worth the complexity. The strong / weak choice is structural; the inner-solver choice (general vs incremental) is performance.

When to switch from IncrementalFourDVar to StrongFourDVar¶

Almost never, for operational work. IncrementalFourDVar is faster and produces the same answer. Use StrongFourDVar only when:

  • BBB is large, unstructured and has no cheap square root, so gaussx.sqrt would fall back to a dense eigendecomposition. Pass a Lanczos square root (prior_sqrt_op=gaussx.sqrt(B, lanczos_order=k)) or use cvt=False before giving up on the incremental solver.

  • You want to swap in a custom optimistix minimiser (e.g. optx.LevenbergMarquardt for very nonlinear cases). Incremental uses a fixed GN+CG; the rest of the optimistix zoo isn’t compatible with the incremental machinery.

For most users, IncrementalFourDVar is the right default.
