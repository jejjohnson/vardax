Observation Model

The observation operator h:RDx→RDyh : \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_y}h:RDx​→RDy​ maps the state x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​ to predicted observations y^=h(x)∈RDy\hat{\vect{y}} = h(\vect{x}) \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y^​=h(x)∈RDy​. Real instruments rarely measure the state directly; they smooth, average, mask or project it. Getting hhh right is what separates an inversion that recovers the truth from one that returns a biased fantasy.

The likelihood model¶

vardax assumes Gaussian observation errors,

y=h(x)+ε, with ε∼N(0,Σε)\vect{y} = h(\vect{x}) + \gvect{\varepsilon}, \text{ with } \gvect{\varepsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon)y=h(x)+ε, with ε∼N(0,Σε​)
(2.1)

where y∈RDy\vect{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y∈RDy​ are the observations, ε∈RDy\gvect{\varepsilon} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}ε∈RDy​ is the observation noise and Σε∈RDy×Dy\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}Σε​∈RDy​×Dy​ is the observation-error covariance, so p(y∣x)=N(y;h(x),Σε)p(\vect{y} \mid \vect{x}) = \mathcal{N}(\vect{y}; h(\vect{x}), \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon)p(y∣x)=N(y;h(x),Σε​). The observation cost in the variational problem is the negative log-likelihood up to a constant,

Jo(x)=12∥y−h(x)∥Σε−12=12(y−h(x))⊤Σε−1(y−h(x))\mathcal{J}_o(\vect{x}) = \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{y} - h(\vect{x}) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}} = \frac{1}{2} (\vect{y} - h(\vect{x}))^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} (\vect{y} - h(\vect{x}))Jo​(x)=21​∥y−h(x)∥Σε−1​2​=21​(y−h(x))⊤Σε−1​(y−h(x))
(2.2)

Non-Gaussian likelihoods (Student-ttt, censored, count data) require either a non-Gaussian extension of Jo\mathcal{J}_oJo​ or amortized inference with a flow / score-based head (chapter 10).

The ObservationOperator protocol¶

Every vardax observation operator satisfies pipekit_cycle.ObservationOperator, restated here with typed signatures:

The shapes are x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​, y^∈RDy\hat{\vect{y}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}y^​∈RDy​ and H∈RDy×Dx\mat{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_x}H∈RDy​×Dx​, the Jacobian of hhh at x\vect{x}x.

The linearize() method returns a lineax.AbstractLinearOperator (typically JacobianLinearOperator via autodiff, but structured overrides are common). The transpose / adjoint comes for free from lineax.

This contract is the same regardless of whether the operator is a masked identity, an averaging kernel, or a multi-instrument fusion.

Five operator families¶

Masked identity¶

In the simplest case we observe the state where the mask is non-zero,

h(x)=m⊙x, with m∈{0,1}Dyh(\vect{x}) = \vect{m} \odot \vect{x}, \text{ with } \vect{m} \in \{0, 1\}^{D_y}h(x)=m⊙x, with m∈{0,1}Dy​
(2.3)

where ⊙\odot⊙ is the element-wise product and Dy=DxD_y = D_xDy​=Dx​.

Tangent-linear: H=M=diag(m)\mat{H} = \mat{M} = \op{diag}(\vect{m})H=M=diag(m), with M∈RDy×Dy\mat{M} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_y}M∈RDy​×Dy​. Use case: SSH altimetry along-track gaps, SST with cloud masks, in-situ samples on a grid.

The mask is passed per call, so the models apply it to H\mat{H}H themselves. The examples on this page build on each other, starting from a toy state on Dx=6D_x = 6Dx​=6 cells.

Linear projection¶

A pre-computed projection from state to observation space is

h(x)=Hxh(\vect{x}) = \mat{H} \vect{x}h(x)=Hx
(2.4)

where H∈RDy×Dx\mat{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_x}H∈RDy​×Dx​ is a fixed matrix.

Use case: Lagrangian footprint matrix from a particle simulator (observation at receptor = footprint-weighted source field), spectral filtering, spatial interpolation. The matrix is stored as an AbstractLinearOperator so structure (sparse, low-rank, Kronecker) can be exploited.

Here the footprint matrix H\mat{H}H maps the six cells to Dy=3D_y = 3Dy​=3 receptors, each averaging two neighbouring cells.

Averaging kernel: RTM L2 products¶

For satellite L2 products derived from optimal-estimation retrievals (TROPOMI CH₄, EMIT CH₄, OCO CO₂, MOPITT CO, …), the L2 retrieval is not a direct sample of the mixing-ratio profile x\vect{x}x. The retrieval applies a smoothing matrix Jx^\mat{J}_{\hat{x}}Jx^​ (the averaging kernel, A\mat{A}A in the retrieval literature) and falls back to a retrieval prior μret\gvect{\mu}_\text{ret}μret​ where the signal is weak:

y^=Jx^(w⊙x+(1−w)⊙μret)\hat{\vect{y}} = \mat{J}_{\hat{x}} (\vect{w} \odot \vect{x} + (1 - \vect{w}) \odot \gvect{\mu}_\text{ret})y^​=Jx^​(w⊙x+(1−w)⊙μret​)
(2.5)

where the symbols are

SymbolDescription
x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​Model state (profile, surface field)
μret∈RDx\gvect{\mu}_\text{ret} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}μret​∈RDx​Retrieval prior from L2 metadata
w∈RDx\vect{w} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}w∈RDx​Weighting vector (often pressure-weighted)
Jx^∈RDx×Dx\mat{J}_{\hat{x}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Jx^​∈RDx​×Dx​Averaging kernel matrix

The retrieved y^\hat{\vect{y}}y^​ lives in the state’s space, so Dy=DxD_y = D_xDy​=Dx​. Tangent-linear: H=Jx^diag(w)\mat{H} = \mat{J}_{\hat{x}} \op{diag}(\vect{w})H=Jx^​diag(w).

Skipping the averaging kernel is the most common cause of bias in operational satellite inversions. Treating an L2 retrieval as a direct measurement of the truth gives systematically wrong posteriors, especially when the retrieval prior μret\gvect{\mu}_\text{ret}μret​ differs from the inversion prior μx\gvect{\mu}_xμx​. vardax exposes AveragingKernel as a first-class operator because this is a day-one requirement, not a future feature.

The toy kernel Jx^\mat{J}_{\hat{x}}Jx^​ below is tridiagonal, the prior μret\gvect{\mu}_\text{ret}μret​ is flat and the sensitivity w\vect{w}w falls from 1 to 0.2 with height. The check of the tangent linear against Jx^diag(w)\mat{J}_{\hat{x}} \op{diag}(\vect{w})Jx^​diag(w) formed densely prints True.

When Jx^\mat{J}_{\hat{x}}Jx^​ has structure (Kronecker for separable kernels, low-rank for under-determined inversions, banded for compactly-supported kernels), store it as a gaussx.AbstractLinearOperator. The tangent linear Jx^diag(w)\mat{J}_{\hat{x}} \op{diag}(\vect{w})Jx^​diag(w) is then computed without ever materialising the dense Jx^\mat{J}_{\hat{x}}Jx^​.

Multi-instrument fusion¶

Operational satellite work combines multiple instruments. Each instrument i=1,…,Ninsti = 1, \ldots, N_\text{inst}i=1,…,Ninst​ has its own hi:RDx→RDy,ih_i : \mathbb{R}^{D_x} \to \mathbb{R}^{D_{y,i}}hi​:RDx​→RDy,i​, mask mi∈{0,1}Dy,i\vect{m}_i \in \{0, 1\}^{D_{y,i}}mi​∈{0,1}Dy,i​, error covariance Σε,i∈RDy,i×Dy,i\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,i} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{y,i} \times D_{y,i}}Σε,i​∈RDy,i​×Dy,i​ and optionally an averaging kernel (Jx^,i,μret,i,wi)(\mat{J}_{\hat{x},i}, \gvect{\mu}_{\text{ret},i}, \vect{w}_i)(Jx^,i​,μret,i​,wi​). The fused observation cost composes (2.2) at the likelihood level,

Jo(x)=∑i=1Ninstαi⋅12∥mi⊙(yi−hi(x))∥Σε,i−12\mathcal{J}_o(\vect{x}) = \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{inst}} \alpha_i \cdot \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{m}_i \odot (\vect{y}_i - h_i(\vect{x})) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,i}^{-1}}Jo​(x)=i=1∑Ninst​​αi​⋅21​∥mi​⊙(yi​−hi​(x))∥Σε,i−1​2​
(2.6)

where NinstN_\text{inst}Ninst​ is the number of instruments and the scalar αi\alpha_iαi​ is a per-instrument weight. The weight defaults to uniform; tuning it is for hierarchical bias correction (Epic 9). Each InstrumentSpec carries its own operator, mask and Σε,i\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,i}Σε,i​. Quality masks zero-weight unreliable pixels, so they contribute zero log-likelihood rather than being dropped, which lets us audit the effective per-instrument observation count.

Any differentiable model: parameter estimation¶

When the control is not the state but a source, a forcing or a parameter vector q∈RDq\vect{q} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_q}q∈RDq​, the observation operator is a downstream model run h(q)h(\vect{q})h(q): a transport model followed by its receptors, a radiative-transfer forward, a neural emulator. The cost J(q)\mathcal{J}(\vect{q})J(q) is then exactly the 3DVar cost (5.1) with x=q\vect{x} = \vect{q}x=q and prior mean μq\gvect{\mu}_qμq​ in place of μx\gvect{\mu}_xμx​, or OI (chapter 4) when hhh is linear. CallableObs wraps any differentiable JAX function as that operator; linearize(x) is a matrix-free lineax.JacobianLinearOperator, so the tangent-linear map is a JVP and its adjoint a VJP.

The observations are noise-free, Σx=I\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \mat{I}Σx​=I and Σε=10−2I\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = 10^{-2} \mat{I}Σε​=10−2I.

Tangent-linear and adjoint contract¶

For incremental 4DVar (chapter 8), every observation operator must expose linearize, whose operator applies H\mat{H}H with mv and H⊤\mat{H}^\topH⊤ with transpose().mv. The pair must pass the adjoint test

⟨Hu,v⟩=⟨u,H⊤v⟩ for all u,v\langle \mat{H} \vect{u}, \vect{v} \rangle = \langle \vect{u}, \mat{H}^\top \vect{v} \rangle \text{ for all } \vect{u}, \vect{v}⟨Hu,v⟩=⟨u,H⊤v⟩ for all u,v
(2.7)

up to floating-point tolerance, where u∈RDx\vect{u} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}u∈RDx​ is a state-space vector and v∈RDy\vect{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}v∈RDy​ an observation-space vector. Failing this test means the gradient flowing back through hhh is wrong, and any downstream optimiser will take wrong steps. The block below draws random u=δx\vect{u} = \delta\vect{x}u=δx and v=r\vect{v} = \vect{r}v=r and prints True; tests/test_obs_operators.py runs the same test for AveragingKernel.

Observation-error covariance Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​¶

Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​ is supplied as a lineax.AbstractLinearOperator. Common parameterisations:

  • Diagonal: lineax.DiagonalLinearOperator(variances). The default for heteroscedastic retrievals (per-pixel σ from L2 metadata).

  • Block-diagonal: gaussx.BlockDiag(*instrument_covs). Per-instrument diagonals when fusing.

  • Structured: kernellib.ImplicitKernelOperator(matern, coords) if observation errors are spatially correlated (rare, but it matters for high-resolution imaging spectrometers).

The models’ solves reject gaussx operators until they are wrapped as a lx.FunctionLinearOperator, as chapter 4 does for Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​.

The choice flows into (2.2) via Σε−1\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}Σε−1​, which is applied lazily: the inverse is never materialised, only applied to vectors via lineax.CG.

When the observation model is wrong¶

A common failure mode: hhh is misspecified (wrong averaging kernel, missing instrument bias, ignored representation error). The MAP x⋆\vect{x}^\starx⋆ then converges to a wrong answer with a confident-looking posterior. Diagnostics:

  • Innovation statistics r=y−h(μx)\vect{r} = \vect{y} - h(\gvect{\mu}_x)r=y−h(μx​). Should be roughly mean-zero and consistent with diag(HΣxH⊤+Σε)\sqrt{\op{diag}(\mat{H} \gmat{\Sigma}_x \mat{H}^\top + \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon)}diag(HΣx​H⊤+Σε​)​. Large systematic offsets indicate bias.

  • Posterior vs prior log-evidence ratio. If the data move the posterior much further than σpost\sigma_\text{post}σpost​ from the prior, the Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ or Σε\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilonΣε​ assumptions are wrong.

  • Cross-instrument disagreement. Per-instrument residual statistics should be consistent; large disagreements flag uncorrected bias.

Epic 9 adds joint bias estimation for the cross-instrument case.
