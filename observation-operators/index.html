Observation Operators

Chapter 2 introduced the observation model (2.1), its cost (2.2) and the ObservationOperator protocol, and it defines each operator family. This chapter is the catalogue of the operators vardax ships (masked identity, linear projection, averaging kernel, multi-instrument fusion and scattered point observations) and of their implementation details.

The first four satisfy pipekit_cycle.ObservationOperator (the first three directly; multi-instrument fusion via its .to_observation_operator() adapter, see chapter 2), which tests/test_pipekit_protocols.py checks. These four expose __call__(x) and linearize(x); the adjoint test at the end of this chapter checks the tangent-linear contract.

The Python blocks on this page run in order, each continuing the ones above it.

Linear projection¶

The linear projection (2.4) applies a stored matrix HmatH_\text{mat}Hmat​. The block builds it from a footprint in which each of M=3M = 3M=3 receptors averages two neighbouring cells.

It prints (3,) True.

Use cases:

  • Lagrangian footprint: a particle simulator produces a footprint matrix mapping the source field to receptor observations. The matrix is often sparse (only cells with non-zero footprint contribute), and lineax has no sparse operator, so wrap the sparse product in a lineax.FunctionLinearOperator.

  • Spectral filtering: observe specific frequency bands. Store Hmat=F−1SFH_\text{mat} = F^{-1} S FHmat​=F−1SF as a composition of lineax operators.

  • Spatial interpolation: bilinear or bicubic projection from the grid to observation points. The interpolation matrix is sparse.

Averaging kernel¶

The averaging kernel (2.5) is the right model for RTM-derived L2 satellite products; chapter 2 defines it and its symbols, and this section covers its construction and structure. For column CH₄ the weighting hhh tends to 1 in the lower troposphere, where the signal comes from the atmosphere, and to 0 in the stratosphere, where the retrieval prior xax_axa​ takes over. The tangent-linear map H′(x)=A⋅diag(h)H'(x) = A \cdot \mathrm{diag}(h)H′(x)=A⋅diag(h) inherits the structure of AAA (sparse, Kronecker, low-rank).

Why skipping the AK is wrong¶

Chapter 2 explains why treating an L2 retrieval as y=x+εy = x + \varepsilony=x+ε instead of (2.5) biases the posterior when xa≠xbx_a \ne x_bxa​=xb​. The retrieval folds in information from xax_axa​ that we then attribute to the data, so the MAP is pulled toward xax_axa​.

The bias is largest when:

  • Information content is low (rank-deficient AAA, large 1−h1-h1−h fraction)

  • xax_axa​ differs from xbx_bxb​ (e.g. inventory prior vs. climatology)

  • Long inversion windows compound the bias

The fix is to always use AveragingKernel. The cost is one extra mat-vec per observation, which is negligible next to the forward-model cost.

It prints {'TROPOMI': (3,), 'EMIT': (6,)} and J_obs at the truth 2.57.

Because the fusion happens at the likelihood level (2.6), nothing is regridded to a common grid. Cross-instrument errors default to independent; correlated errors need a block-non-diagonal RRR. The quality mask mim_imi​ lives in each InstrumentSpec, since Batch* has no per-pixel instrument index.

Cross-instrument bias¶

Real instruments disagree systematically. TROPOMI and GHGSat differ by about 5 to 20 ppb on methane retrievals due to spectral calibration, retrieval algorithm differences, and footprint effects. Pretending they agree biases the joint analysis.

For joint inversion with bias estimation (Epic 9), introduce a per-instrument bias state bib_ibi​, which shifts each prediction to Hi(x)+biH_i(x) + b_iHi​(x)+bi​ and adds a Gaussian prior cost on bib_ibi​:

It prints J_b = 0.125.

The bias bib_ibi​ becomes a joint state variable, and the analysis solves for (x,b)(x, \boldsymbol{b})(x,b) together. The bias prior tames the underdeterminacy. Hierarchical priors per (instrument, basin, season) are the operational pattern.

This is not yet shipped in vardax; the design is documented for Epic 9.

Scattered point observations¶

Along-track altimetry, drifters and station networks deliver observations at scattered coordinates and times, not on the state grid. Scattering them into a mostly-NaN grid would discard their positions and force one RRR for all times. Two operators evaluate H(x)\mathcal H(x)H(x) at the points:

  • GridPointObs(grid_axes): for a gridded state. The state has a leading time axis, either a 4D-Var trajectory (x0,…,xT)(x_0, \dots, x_T)(x0​,…,xT​) or a 3D-Var state (T, N). This is InterpObs’s order=1 stencil, computed at call time so that the coordinates may be traced.
