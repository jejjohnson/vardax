Adjoint Methods

Every analysis method that involves minimisation or dynamics needs gradients. For 4DVar, that’s gradients through the ODE rollout. For FourDVarNet, that’s gradients through the inner learned solver. For training amortized heads, it’s standard backprop.

Vardax does not own adjoint code. Gradients through dynamics come from diffrax.AbstractAdjoint; gradients through inner minimisers come from optimistix.AbstractAdjoint (Decision D15). This chapter explains the choices and when to use which.

Why composable adjoints¶

Historical DA libraries hand-coded adjoint models: every forward operator MtM_tMt​ required a hand-written companion Mt⊤M^\top_tMt⊤​, often generated by automatic-differentiation source-to-source transformations (TAMC, OpenAD), and maintaining the adjoint code as the forward evolved was a significant engineering tax.

JAX changed the picture by making reverse-mode autodiff a first-class language feature. diffrax and optimistix build on this by exposing named adjoint strategies as user-selectable types. The user chooses between memory and time, between exact and approximate; the library handles the differentiation.

Vardax exposes these strategies as constructor slots on the Layer 2 models, with no vardax-owned grad_mode enum and no in-house adjoint implementation.

Adjoints through dynamics (diffrax.AbstractAdjoint)¶

For 4DVar with dynamics, the cost is the strong-constraint cost (6.1), and its gradient (6.2) pulls the observation misfits back through the adjoint (Mt′)⊤(M'_t)^\top(Mt′​)⊤ of every step of the rollout. diffrax.diffeqsolve(..., adjoint=...) computes that pull-back, and there are four choices.

RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint(checkpoints=N), the default¶

Recursive checkpointing of the forward trajectory. Stores N∼log⁡KN \sim \log KN∼logK checkpoints; recomputes intermediate states from checkpoints during the backward pass.

MemoryTimeWhen
O(log⁡K)O(\log K)O(logK)O(Klog⁡K)O(K \log K)O(KlogK)Default. Balanced.

The default N=⌈log⁡2K⌉+1N = \lceil \log_2 K \rceil + 1N=⌈log2​K⌉+1 is usually right. Override only if profiling shows memory pressure.

BacksolveAdjoint(), the continuous adjoint¶

Solve the adjoint ODE backwards in time. The adjoint state λ(t)\lambda(t)λ(t) satisfies

dλdt=−(M′(x(t)))⊤λ(t)−∇x(t)J\frac{d\lambda}{dt} = -(M'(x(t)))^\top \lambda(t) - \nabla_{x(t)} Jdtdλ​=−(M′(x(t)))⊤λ(t)−∇x(t)​J
(12.1)

where M′(x(t))M'(x(t))M′(x(t)) is the Jacobian of the vector field along the forward trajectory and ∇x(t)J\nabla_{x(t)} J∇x(t)​J is the forcing from the observation misfit at time ttt. It is integrated from t=Tt = Tt=T to t=0t = 0t=0 and is the continuous-time counterpart of the discrete recursion (6.5): read backwards in time, the misfit forcing adds to λ\lambdaλ. Because the forward state is recomputed during that backward solve, the memory is constant in the length of the trajectory.

MemoryTimeWhen
O(1)O(1)O(1)∼2×\sim 2 \times∼2× forwardLong windows, memory-constrained

Caveat: reverse-time stiffness. If the forward ODE is well-conditioned with an explicit integrator, the backward might be stiff. Use dfx.Kvaerno5 or dfx.ImplicitEuler for the adjoint if so.

ForwardMode(), forward sensitivity¶

Compute gradients with forward-mode autodiff, which solves the sensitivity ODEs forward in time alongside the state.

MemoryTimeWhen
O(P)O(P)O(P)O(P)×O(P) \timesO(P)× forwardFew parameters (PPP small)

Use it when you differentiate with respect to a small number of parameters (e.g. a single emission rate QQQ in methane Tier I). Forward mode wins when P≪NP \ll NP≪N (the state dimension); reverse mode wins when P≫1P \gg 1P≫1.

DirectAdjoint(), plain reverse¶

Standard reverse-mode autodiff through the lax.scan of integration steps. No checkpointing.

MemoryTimeWhen
O(K)O(K)O(K)O(K)O(K)O(K)Short windows; debugging

Slow but transparent. It is useful when debugging adjoint issues, because the gradient is literally the reverse-mode autodiff result with no clever tricks.

Adjoints through inner minimisers (optimistix.AbstractAdjoint)¶

For methods that train through an inner minimisation (FourDVarNet, AmortizedPosterior, or any classical method whose hyperparameters are themselves trained), the training gradient flows through optimistix.minimise(...) via its adjoint argument.

RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint()¶

Unroll the inner iteration with checkpointing. Standard backprop through the iterative solver. Used by FourDVarNet for end-to-end training when memory permits.

MemoryConvergence requirement
O(K)O(K)O(K) checkpointsNone

ImplicitAdjoint(), the implicit function theorem at the optimum¶

At the optimum x∗x^*x∗ of min⁡xf(x;θ)\min_x f(x; \theta)minx​f(x;θ), the implicit function theorem gives

dx∗dθ=−(∇x2f(x∗;θ))−1∇xθ2f(x∗;θ)\frac{d x^*}{d \theta} = -\left( \nabla^2_x f(x^*; \theta) \right)^{-1} \nabla^2_{x \theta} f(x^*; \theta)dθdx∗​=−(∇x2​f(x∗;θ))−1∇xθ2​f(x∗;θ)
(12.2)

where fff is the inner objective (a cost such as (5.1)), xxx the inner control and θ\thetaθ the outer parameters being trained. optimistix solves this system implicitly: it never inverts the Hessian, and uses lineax to apply (∇x2f)−1(\nabla^2_x f)^{-1}(∇x2​f)−1 to vectors.

MemoryConvergence requirement
O(1)O(1)O(1)Yes (must reach optimum)

Used by classical methods (ThreeDVar, StrongFourDVar, WeakFourDVar) when training hyperparameters. The exact-at-optimum property is the right model here, since at a well-converged MAP (12.2) gives the correct sensitivity.

DirectAdjoint()¶

optimistix ships no DirectAdjoint; its only adjoints are RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint and ImplicitAdjoint. Plain reverse through the solver loop is optx.RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint(), and to_optimistix_adjoint rejects a DirectAdjoint spec for that reason.

vardax.adjoints.OneStepAdjoint() (Bolte et al. 2023)¶

A custom vardax-owned adjoint.

MemoryConvergence requirement
O(1)O(1)O(1)Approximately convergent

It runs K−1K-1K−1 inner iterations under jax.lax.stop_gradient, then a single differentiable step, so the training gradient picks up only the last step’s contribution. Bolte et al. prove this is exact at the fixed point (the fixed-point gradient (9.4)); in practice it gives near-correct gradients for converged inner solvers and reduces memory a lot.

The example builds a FourDVarNet1D for S=2S = 2S=2 windows of T=4T = 4T=4 times and N=8N = 8N=8 cells, fed with standard-normal observations yyy, and runs its K=15K = 15K=15 learned solver steps Φθ\Phi_\thetaΦθ​ (the update (9.2)) under OneStepAdjoint. Slots and where they apply¶

Vardax models that involve both dynamics and inner minimisation carry separate slots for the two:

SlotUsed byDefault
checkpoint: bool | intStrongFourDVar, WeakFourDVar, IncrementalFourDVar, ReducedBasisFourDVarFalse (plain reverse mode)
forward_adjoint: diffrax.AbstractAdjointthe same four; needs a forward with with_adjoint (SupportsForwardAdjoint)None (the forward’s own)
minimiser_adjoint: optimistix.AbstractAdjointThreeDVar, StrongFourDVar, WeakFourDVar, ReducedBasisFourDVarImplicitAdjoint()
solver_adjoint: optimistix.AbstractAdjointFourDVarNet1D, FourDVarNet2D (through the learned inner solver)RecursiveCheckpointAdjoint()

checkpoint and forward_adjoint act on the dynamics rollout; minimiser_adjoint and solver_adjoint act on the inner minimisation. They are independent.

  • checkpoint=True wraps each step of the rollout in jax.checkpoint (an integer k wraps blocks of k steps), so reverse mode recomputes the steps instead of storing their intermediates. It works for any per-step forward and leaves the gradient unchanged.

  • forward_adjoint= is handed to forward.with_adjoint(adjoint), which a diffrax-backed forward implements by passing the adjoint to diffeqsolve (chapter 3). With a forward that has no with_adjoint, the model raises ValueError at construction rather than ignoring the slot.

Disagreement is a bug. The most common causes are reverse-time instability in BacksolveAdjoint (switch to an implicit integrator) and under-converged optimisation in ImplicitAdjoint (tighten the tolerance).

The shared strategy vocabulary¶

The same concepts recur at every layer, so the stack shares one declarative vocabulary: the frozen-dataclass specs in pipekit_cycle.adjoints (named after their diffrax counterparts). Each layer interprets a spec into its native mechanism, which is pipekit_jax.to_diffrax_adjoint at the dynamics layer, to_optimistix_adjoint at the inner-solve layer (this chapter), and filterax’s scan strategies at the cycle layer:

Specdynamics (diffrax)inner solve (optimistix / vardax)cycle (filterax scan)
DirectAdjointdiffrax.DirectAdjointunrolled (the default)plain scan
RecursiveCheckpointAdjointdiffrax.RecursiveCheckpointAdjointoptx.RecursiveCheckpointAdjointcheckpointed scan
TruncatedAdjoint(k)noneKStepAdjoint(k); k=1 ≡ OneStepAdjointtruncated scan
ImplicitAdjointsteady-state onlyoptx.ImplicitAdjointnone
BacksolveAdjointdiffrax.BacksolveAdjoint ⚠ unstable for chaotic/stiff dynamicsnonenone

The spec TruncatedAdjoint(k=3) maps to KStepAdjoint(k=3), which keeps the last three of the solver’s KKK steps of (9.2) differentiable. The block continues the first one on the page.

It prints KStepAdjoint(k=3) (2, 4, 8).

KStepAdjoint generalises the one-step strategy: at a converged fixed point any kkk is exact (the warmup contributes nothing); for the truncated, unconverged solvers of 4DVarNet, larger kkk trades memory for a less biased gradient. The dynamics layer composes independently: a pipekit_jax.DiffraxForwardModel slots into StrongFourDVar as a plain pipekit_cycle.ForwardModel, bringing diffrax’s solvers and adjoints to the forecast step with no vardax code changes.
