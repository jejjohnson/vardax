Lorenz Examples

The Lorenz systems are the canonical small-scale testbeds for DA. Their chaotic dynamics expose what each method does well or badly in a setting where ground truth is available, integration is cheap, and the state dimension is small enough to visualise.

This chapter walks the seven analysis methods through Lorenz-63 (3D chaotic system) and Lorenz-96 (40D periodic system). The goal is to make each method’s behaviour concrete.

Lorenz-63¶

The Lorenz-63 system is

x˙=σ(y−x),y˙=x(ρ−z)−y,z˙=xy−βz,\dot{x} = \sigma(y - x), \dot{y} = x(\rho - z) - y, \dot{z} = xy - \beta z,x˙=σ(y−x),y˙​=x(ρ−z)−y,z˙=xy−βz,
(15.1)

where (x,y,z)(x, y, z)(x,y,z) are the three components of the state xt∈RDx\vect{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}xt​∈RDx​ with Dx=3D_x = 3Dx​=3, and σ\sigmaσ, ρ\rhoρ, β∈R\beta \in \mathbb{R}β∈R are its three dimensionless parameters, here the canonical (10,28,8/3)(10, 28, 8/3)(10,28,8/3). These three letters are the physics symbols of Lorenz-63, not a standard deviation, a density or a regularisation weight; the standard deviation of the observation noise is written σo\sigma_oσo​. The dynamics live on the famous two-winged attractor.

Synthetic observation scenario¶

Take one window of T=100T = 100T=100 steps, which holds T+1=101T + 1 = 101T+1=101 times (one time unit, about one Lyapunov time), and observe it every tenth step, with Gaussian noise of standard deviation σo=1\sigma_o = 1σo​=1: yt=xt+εt\vect{y}_t = \vect{x}_t + \gvect{\varepsilon}_tyt​=xt​+εt​ with εt∼N(0,Σε)\gvect{\varepsilon}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon)εt​∼N(0,Σε​), Σε=σo2I\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = \sigma_o^2 \mat{I}Σε​=σo2​I and Dy=DxD_y = D_xDy​=Dx​. The data utilities are private helpers under vardax._src.utils, which the tutorial notebooks also use. xr_to_batch1d expects a patch dimension, so the window gets one.

OptimalInterpolation won’t work directly¶

OI (chapter 4) assumes a static Gaussian field, which the nonlinear Lorenz dynamics violate. So OI with the climatological mean xˉ\bar{\vect{x}}xˉ and covariance Σ^x\hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_xΣ^x​ of the trajectory as its prior gives a poor reconstruction, but it is a useful baseline. Each time is treated separately, with μx=xˉ\gvect{\mu}_x = \bar{\vect{x}}μx​=xˉ, Σx=Σ^x\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \hat{\gmat{\Sigma}}_xΣx​=Σ^x​ and Σε=I\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = \mat{I}Σε​=I.

It prints OI trajectory RMSE 6.06.

StrongFourDVar is the right tool¶

The background μx\gvect{\mu}_xμx​ is the true initial state xtrue\vect{x}_\text{true}xtrue​ of the window plus noise of standard deviation σb=2\sigma_b = 2σb​=2, and the prior Σx=100I\gmat{\Sigma}_x = 100 \mat{I}Σx​=100I is deliberately weak. Ht\mat{H}_tHt​ is the masked identity and Σε=I\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon = \mat{I}Σε​=I.

It prints strong 4DVar trajectory RMSE 0.28.

Diagnostic: plot the attractor reconstruction. StrongFourDVar recovers the butterfly; OptimalInterpolation smears across both wings because the static prior doesn’t know about the dynamics.

FourDVarNet for the learned-prior comparison¶

Train a small AE prior on short patches of the training trajectory. Two epochs of vardax.examples.fit_demo only show the plumbing; notebook 03 trains it properly.

It prints (8, 20, 3).

FourDVarNet1D learns the attractor structure from the training data and reconstructs without explicit access to the dynamics. The comparison strong_analysis vs net_analysis is the canonical demonstration that learned priors recover similar information to model-based ones.

Lorenz-96¶

The Lorenz-96 system is a one-dimensional periodic version:

x˙k=(xk+1−xk−2)xk−1−xk+u, for k=0,…,Dx−1,\dot{x}_k = (x_{k+1} - x_{k-2}) x_{k-1} - x_k + u, \text{ for } k = 0, \ldots, D_x - 1,x˙k​=(xk+1​−xk−2​)xk−1​−xk​+u, for k=0,…,Dx​−1,
(15.2)

where xkx_kxk​ is the component at site kkk of the state x∈RDx\vect{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x∈RDx​, a periodic ring (indices taken modulo DxD_xDx​), and uuu is the forcing. We use Dx=40D_x = 40Dx​=40, which replaces the Lorenz-63 value of 3 until the linear-Gaussian test below, and u=8u = 8u=8, which is the chaotic regime. The state is a periodic 1D field, closer in structure to atmospheric problems than Lorenz-63.

It prints (5001, 40).

IncrementalFourDVar with structured Matérn prior¶

This is the realistic operational scenario: structured spatial prior, incremental solver (chapter 8), AK-style or masked observations.

The Matérn-3/2 covariance on the periodic grid with length scale ℓ=4\ell = 4ℓ=4 sites and unit variance depends only on the ring distance dij=min⁡(∣i−j∣,Dx−∣i−j∣)d_{ij} = \min(\vert i - j \vert, D_x - \vert i - j \vert)dij​=min(∣i−j∣,Dx​−∣i−j∣) between sites iii and jjj, so it is circulant; here it is a dense Dx×DxD_x \times D_xDx​×Dx​ matrix. gaussx.Toeplitz would store it by its first column; the dense matrix keeps the example short. The forward model is one RK4 step written on the page, which forward- and reverse-mode autodiff both see through, as the Gauss-Newton loop needs. The background is the true initial state plus noise drawn from the same scale as Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​.

It prints background error 5.76, analysis error 2.98 and (40,), the errors being in x0\vect{x}_0x0​.

This walkthrough exercises the periodic Matérn prior as a lineax operator, the CVT (8.6) with Σx1/2\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{1/2}Σx1/2​ from the Matérn factorisation, and the Gauss-Newton and CG loop (8.5), whose Hessian (8.4) the posterior reuses for free.

The reconstruction recovers the Lorenz-96 trajectory through gaps, with calibrated posterior uncertainty in unobserved regions.

Linear-Gaussian agreement test¶

Lorenz dynamics aren’t linear, but we can construct a static linear-Gaussian test on the Lorenz-63 attractor by treating the trajectory mean as the prior mean and the empirical covariance as Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​.

With f(x)=xf(\vect{x}) = \vect{x}f(x)=x over one time, the BLUE mean (4.4) and the minimisers of (5.1), (6.1) and (8.3) are the same point. Only the four classical methods take this batch; FourDVarNet is left out, as in tests/test_linear_gaussian_agreement.py, because its learned components must be trained before they can match.

It prints agrees with OI: True for all four methods.

This is the Decision D14 invariant in action.
