18 · Latent Variational Data Assimilation

This chapter formalises variational data assimilation in a learned low-dimensional latent space. It complements chapters 5 to 8 (3DVar, strong and weak 4DVar, incremental 4DVar) and chapter 9 (4DVarNet) by specifying three latent-space peers: latent 3DVar, latent strong-4DVar, and latent hybrid 4DVar.

Design anchor: vardax × Latent Data Assimilation, Decision D17.

18.2 Latent 3DVar¶

18.2.1 Cost¶

The latent counterpart of the 3DVar cost (5.1) replaces the control vector x\vect{x}x with z\vect{z}z, the observation operator hhh with hzh_zhz​ (so x=gdec(z)\vect{x} = g_\text{dec}(\vect{z})x=gdec​(z) in the misfit) and the background term with one in z\vect{z}z:

J3D(z)=12∥z−μz∥Σz−12⏟background, in z+12∥y−h(gdec(z))∥Σε−12⏟obs misfit, lifted,\mathcal{J}_\text{3D}(\vect{z}) = \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{z} - \gvect{\mu}_z \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}}}_{\text{background, in } \vect{z}} + \underbrace{\frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{y} - h(g_\text{dec}(\vect{z})) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}}}_{\text{obs misfit, lifted}},J3D​(z)=background, in z21​∥z−μz​∥Σz−1​2​​​+obs misfit, lifted21​∥y−h(gdec​(z))∥Σε−1​2​​​,
(18.1)

where μz\gvect{\mu}_zμz​ is the background in z\vect{z}z.

∇zJ3D(z)=Σz−1(z−μz)+Hz(z)⊤Σε−1(hz(z)−y),\nabla_{\vect{z}} \mathcal{J}_\text{3D}(\vect{z}) = \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}(\vect{z} - \gvect{\mu}_z) + \mat{H}_z(\vect{z})^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}(h_z(\vect{z}) - \vect{y}),∇z​J3D​(z)=Σz−1​(z−μz​)+Hz​(z)⊤Σε−1​(hz​(z)−y),
(18.2)

and the Gauss-Newton Hessian (5.3) becomes

∇z2J3D(z)≈Σz−1+Hz(z)⊤Σε−1Hz(z).\nabla^2_{\vect{z}} \mathcal{J}_\text{3D}(\vect{z}) \approx \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} + \mat{H}_z(\vect{z})^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \mat{H}_z(\vect{z}).∇z2​J3D​(z)≈Σz−1​+Hz​(z)⊤Σε−1​Hz​(z).

When Hz\mat{H}_zHz​ is treated as constant near the linearisation point (the incremental form, cf. chapter 8), the minimisation reduces to a linear solve and the Sherman-Morrison-Woodbury identity gives the closed form

This is the BLUE mean (4.4) with (μx,Σx,H)(\gvect{\mu}_x, \gmat{\Sigma}_x, \mat{H})(μx​,Σx​,H) replaced by (μz,Σz,Hz)(\gvect{\mu}_z, \gmat{\Sigma}_z, \mat{H}_z)(μz​,Σz​,Hz​): the Dy×DyD_y \times D_yDy​×Dy​ inverse matches the OI form, and the difference is that the gain is now multiplied by Σz\gmat{\Sigma}_zΣz​ (size Dz×DzD_z \times D_zDz​×Dz​) rather than Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ (size Dx×DxD_x \times D_xDx​×Dx​). This is the structural source of the order-of-magnitude wall-clock win when Dz≪DxD_z \ll D_xDz​≪Dx​.

18.2.3 Analysis output¶

The variational solution is in z\vect{z}z; the analysis in physical space is x⋆=gdec(z⋆)\vect{x}^\star = g_\text{dec}(\vect{z}^\star)x⋆=gdec​(z⋆). The posterior covariance, when needed, comes from the Laplace approximation (13.1) at z⋆\vect{z}^\starz⋆, pushed forward through the decoder:

(18.5)

The pushforward Σx∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}Σx∣y​ has rank at most DzD_zDz​, so it is a low-rank object naturally represented by lineax.LowRankUpdate.

18.3 Latent strong-constraint 4DVar¶

18.3.1 Cost¶

The latent strong-constraint cost is (6.1) with the control z0\vect{z}_0z0​, the rollout zt+1=fz(zt)\vect{z}_{t+1} = f_z(\vect{z}_t)zt+1​=fz​(zt​) in the latent space, and the observation residuals through the lifted operator:

J4D(z0)=12∥z0−μz∥Σz−12+12∑t=0T∥yt−h(gdec(zt))∥Σε−12, with zt+1=fz(zt),\mathcal{J}_\text{4D}(\vect{z}_0) = \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{z}_0 - \gvect{\mu}_z \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Vert \vect{y}_t - h(g_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_t)) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}}, \text{ with } \vect{z}_{t+1} = f_z(\vect{z}_t),J4D​(z0​)=21​∥z0​−μz​∥Σz−1​2​+21​t=0∑T​∥yt​−h(gdec​(zt​))∥Σε−1​2​, with zt+1​=fz​(zt​),
(18.6)

where t=0,…,Tt = 0, \ldots, Tt=0,…,T indexes the observation times of the window, which has TTT steps and T+1T + 1T+1 times, and yt∈RDy\vect{y}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y}yt​∈RDy​.

18.3.2 Gradient¶

Backpropagation through the latent rollout gives the analogue of the strong 4DVar gradient (6.2),

∇z0J4D=Σz−1(z0−μz)+∑t=0T(∏j=0t−1Fz(zj))⊤Hz(zt)⊤Σε−1(hz(zt)−yt).\nabla_{\vect{z}_0} \mathcal{J}_\text{4D} = \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}(\vect{z}_0 - \gvect{\mu}_z) + \sum_{t=0}^{T} \left(\prod_{j=0}^{t-1} \mat{F}_z(\vect{z}_j)\right)^\top \mat{H}_z(\vect{z}_t)^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} (h_z(\vect{z}_t) - \vect{y}_t).∇z0​​J4D​=Σz−1​(z0​−μz​)+t=0∑T​(j=0∏t−1​Fz​(zj​))⊤Hz​(zt​)⊤Σε−1​(hz​(zt​)−yt​).
(18.7)

For learned fzf_zfz​ implemented as an eqx.Module, the tangent-linear operators come from JAX autodiff; no hand-coded adjoint is required.

18.3.3 Memory bound¶

The full unrolled adjoint stores O(TDz)O(T D_z)O(TDz​) values rather than O(TDx)O(T D_x)O(TDx​), another order-of-magnitude saving for large DxD_xDx​. With T=20T = 20T=20, Dz=8D_z = 8Dz​=8 versus Dx=104D_x = 10^4Dx​=104, the backward tape shrinks from megabytes to kilobytes.

18.4 Latent hybrid 4DVar¶

18.4.1 Motivation¶

The strong-latent form (§18.3) commits the forecast to the latent manifold, which can accumulate reconstruction error over long windows. The hybrid form keeps the forecast in physical space, where the trusted physics model lives, and uses z\vect{z}z only as the control variable and as the natural background space:

  • Control: z0∈RDz\vect{z}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z}z0​∈RDz​.

  • Initial physical state: x0=gdec(z0)\vect{x}_0 = g_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_0)x0​=gdec​(z0​).

  • Rollout: xt+1=fx(xt)\vect{x}_{t+1} = f_x(\vect{x}_t)xt+1​=fx​(xt​) in RDx\mathbb{R}^{D_x}RDx​ (physics, not AE).

  • Observation residual: yt−h(xt)\vect{y}_t - h(\vect{x}_t)yt​−h(xt​), no gdecg_\text{dec}gdec​ inside the sum.

18.4.2 Cost¶

The hybrid cost is the strong 4DVar cost (6.1) with x0=gdec(z0)\vect{x}_0 = g_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_0)x0​=gdec​(z0​) and the background term moved to z\vect{z}z:

Jhyb(z0)=12∥z0−μz∥Σz−12+12∑t=0T∥yt−h(xt)∥Σε−12, with x0=gdec(z0),xt+1=fx(xt).\mathcal{J}_\text{hyb}(\vect{z}_0) = \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{z}_0 - \gvect{\mu}_z \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Vert \vect{y}_t - h(\vect{x}_t) \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}}, \text{ with } \vect{x}_0 = g_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_0), \vect{x}_{t+1} = f_x(\vect{x}_t).Jhyb​(z0​)=21​∥z0​−μz​∥Σz−1​2​+21​t=0∑T​∥yt​−h(xt​)∥Σε−1​2​, with x0​=gdec​(z0​),xt+1​=fx​(xt​).
(18.8)

18.4.3 Gradient¶

∇z0Jhyb=Σz−1(z0−μz)+Jdec(z0)⊤∑t=0T(∏j=0t−1Fx(xj))⊤H(xt)⊤Σε−1(h(xt)−yt).\nabla_{\vect{z}_0} \mathcal{J}_\text{hyb} = \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}(\vect{z}_0 - \gvect{\mu}_z) + \mat{J}_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_0)^\top \sum_{t=0}^{T} \left(\prod_{j=0}^{t-1} \mat{F}_x(\vect{x}_j)\right)^\top \mat{H}(\vect{x}_t)^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}(h(\vect{x}_t) - \vect{y}_t).∇z0​​Jhyb​=Σz−1​(z0​−μz​)+Jdec​(z0​)⊤t=0∑T​(j=0∏t−1​Fx​(xj​))⊤H(xt​)⊤Σε−1​(h(xt​)−yt​).
(18.9)

The decoder Jacobian Jdec(z0)\mat{J}_\text{dec}(\vect{z}_0)Jdec​(z0​) appears only once, at the start of the window. The expensive piece is the physics adjoint ∏Fx\prod \mat{F}_x∏Fx​, identical to the one in the strong 4DVar gradient (6.2).

18.5 Latent incremental 4DVar (sketch)¶

The incremental algorithm of chapter 8 extends mutatis mutandis: the outer Gauss-Newton loop produces a sequence of inner problems, each the incremental cost (8.3) in z\vect{z}z,

min⁡δz12∥z0(i)+δz−μz∥Σz−12+12∑t=0T∥rt(i)−Hz,tFz,t←0δz∥Σε−12,\min_{\delta\vect{z}} \frac{1}{2} \Vert \vect{z}_0^{(i)} + \delta\vect{z} - \gvect{\mu}_z \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1}} + \frac{1}{2} \sum_{t=0}^{T} \Vert \vect{r}_t^{(i)} - \mat{H}_{z,t} \mat{F}_{z,t \leftarrow 0} \delta\vect{z} \Vert^2_{\gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1}},δzmin​21​∥z0(i)​+δz−μz​∥Σz−1​2​+21​t=0∑T​∥rt(i)​−Hz,t​Fz,t←0​δz∥Σε−1​2​,
(18.10)

with z0(i)\vect{z}_0^{(i)}z0(i)​ the outer guess at outer iteration iii, rt(i)=yt−hz(zt(i))\vect{r}_t^{(i)} = \vect{y}_t - h_z(\vect{z}_t^{(i)})rt(i)​=yt​−hz​(zt(i)​) the residual (innovation), Fz,t←0∈RDz×Dz\mat{F}_{z,t \leftarrow 0} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z \times D_z}Fz,t←0​∈RDz​×Dz​ the tangent-linear model of the latent rollout to time ttt and Hz,t\mat{H}_{z,t}Hz,t​ the lifted Jacobian at zt(i)\vect{z}_t^{(i)}zt(i)​, both evaluated at the current outer guess. The inner problem is linear-quadratic in δz\delta\vect{z}δz and CG-able in RDz\mathbb{R}^{D_z}RDz​, typically with two orders of magnitude fewer CG iterations than in RDx\mathbb{R}^{D_x}RDx​ because the conditioning improves with dimensionality reduction.

A control-variable transform (CVT) in z\vect{z}z is rarely needed: if the AE was trained with a unit-Gaussian prior on z\vect{z}z, then Σz=I\gmat{\Sigma}_z = \mat{I}Σz​=I to leading order and the cost is already well-conditioned.

18.6 Identity-AE consistency¶

If (genc,gdec)=(id,id)(g_\text{enc}, g_\text{dec}) = (\mathrm{id}, \mathrm{id})(genc​,gdec​)=(id,id) then z=x\vect{z} = \vect{x}z=x, hz=hh_z = hhz​=h, fz=fxf_z = f_xfz​=fx​, Σz=Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_z = \gmat{\Sigma}_xΣz​=Σx​, and the three latent costs reduce exactly to the corresponding x\vect{x}x-space costs. This is the canonical regression test for any implementation (vardax.identity_latent_map(N)).

18.7 Connection to chapter 9 (4DVarNet)¶

FourDVarNet minimises the learned-prior cost (9.1) with the prior gae=gdec∘gencg_\text{ae} = g_\text{dec} \circ g_\text{enc}gae​=gdec​∘genc​, so its prior term is αp∥x−gdec(genc(x))∥22\alpha_p \Vert \vect{x} - g_\text{dec}(g_\text{enc}(\vect{x})) \Vert_2^2αp​∥x−gdec​(genc​(x))∥22​.

This is prior-only latent: the AE appears as a reconstruction penalty but the control remains x\vect{x}x. The latent methods of this chapter promote z\vect{z}z itself to the control vector. The two are not incompatible, and FourDVarNet retains its place when the autoencoder is a regulariser rather than a parameterisation of the state space.

18.8 Worked example: Lorenz-96, Dx=40D_x = 40Dx​=40, Dz=8D_z = 8Dz​=8¶

The notebook docs/notebooks/18_latent_lorenz.ipynb (to be added with the implementation) trains a bilinear AE on L96 trajectories and runs all three latent methods against the x\vect{x}x-space baselines on the same fixture. Expected pattern:

  • LatentThreeDVar matches ThreeDVar analysis RMSE to within 0.05 standard deviations; cost-per-cycle drops by ∼25×\sim 25\times∼25×.

  • LatentStrongFourDVar with EncodedForwardModel (no learned fzf_zfz​) matches StrongFourDVar; cost-per-cycle drops by ∼10×\sim 10\times∼10× (the AE round-trip eats some of the win).

  • LatentStrongFourDVar with a trained residual-MLP fzf_zfz​ wins ∼50×\sim 50\times∼50× but introduces a small RMSE bias controlled by AE reconstruction quality.

  • LatentHybridFourDVar matches StrongFourDVar RMSE almost exactly; cost-per-cycle drops by ∼4×\sim 4\times∼4× (dominated by physics rollout which is unchanged).
