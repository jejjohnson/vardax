Posterior Covariance

A MAP estimate x⋆\vect{x}^\starx⋆ is the start of the answer, not the end. Real DA needs a posterior (mean, covariance and provenance) for downstream consumers (population models, decision pipelines, alerting systems) to propagate uncertainty correctly. This chapter covers the four posterior-covariance computations vardax supports and when to use which; the method chapters point here for that choice.

The Posterior container¶

Every analysis emits a Posterior(mean, cov, samples, provenance), plus the pieces of a linear control when it has one:

The shapes are μx∣y∈RDx\gvect{\mu}_{x \mid y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}μx∣y​∈RDx​ (the state’s own shape, flattened), Σx∣y∈RDx×Dx\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Σx∣y​∈RDx​×Dx​, x(e)∈RDx\vect{x}^{(e)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x(e)∈RDx​ for the NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ members e∈Ee \in \mathcal{E}e∈E, and, for a linear control with DzD_zDz​ coefficients, Φ∈RDx×Dz\gmat{\Phi} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_z}Φ∈RDx​×Dz​ and Σz∣y∈RDz×Dz\gmat{\Sigma}_{z \mid y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z \times D_z}Σz∣y​∈RDz​×Dz​. The covariance is an AbstractLinearOperator and is never materialised. The mat-vec Σx∣yv\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y} \vect{v}Σx∣y​v and the diagonal diag(Σx∣y)\op{diag}(\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y})diag(Σx∣y​) are computed on demand without building the dense Σx∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}Σx∣y​.

posterior.sample(key, n_samples) draws x(e)=μx∣y+Σx∣y1/2ξ(e)\vect{x}^{(e)} = \gvect{\mu}_{x \mid y} + \gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}^{1/2} \gvect{\xi}^{(e)}x(e)=μx∣y​+Σx∣y1/2​ξ(e) with ξ(e)∼N(0,I)\gvect{\xi}^{(e)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mat{I})ξ(e)∼N(0,I). The draws come from gaussx.sample_mvn, which picks the square root by the structure of Σx∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}Σx∣y​ (an FFT circulant embedding for Toeplitz, a block-banded Cholesky for BlockTriDiag, closed forms for other structured operators) and falls back to the symmetric square root, so a singular Σx∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}Σx∣y​ still has finite gradients. When the posterior came from a linear control (a LinearParameterization, ReducedBasisFourDVar(basis=...) or IncrementalFourDVar’s whitened control), it draws in coefficient space instead, x(e)=μx∣y+ΦΣz∣y1/2ξ(e)\vect{x}^{(e)} = \gvect{\mu}_{x \mid y} + \gmat{\Phi} \gmat{\Sigma}_{z \mid y}^{1/2} \gvect{\xi}^{(e)}x(e)=μx∣y​+ΦΣz∣y1/2​ξ(e), which only factors the small Σz∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{z \mid y}Σz∣y​. A posterior that already holds samples returns a random subset of them.

OptimalInterpolation and IncrementalFourDVar produce posteriors as part of the analysis call (closed-form / GN-Hessian reuse). The other five methods pair with an explicit PosteriorAdapter.

Four posterior families¶

1. Closed form (BLUE / OI)¶

For OptimalInterpolation, the posterior is exact: Σx∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}Σx∣y​ is the BLUE covariance (4.5).

The examples on this page share one toy problem. The state xt∈RDx\vect{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}xt​∈RDx​ has Dx=6D_x = 6Dx​=6 cells on a ring, and the forward model averages each cell with its right neighbour, xt=Fxt−1\vect{x}_t = \mat{F} \vect{x}_{t-1}xt​=Fxt−1​ with F=12(I+P)\mat{F} = \frac{1}{2}(\mat{I} + \mat{P})F=21​(I+P) and P\mat{P}P the cyclic shift. The cov operator is lazy: each mat-vec solves the innovation system the analysis solved, with the same solver, and never applies Σx−1\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1}Σx−1​, so a smooth Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ that CG cannot invert is no obstacle. This is the cheapest and most accurate posterior, when it applies.

2. Laplace approximation¶

For ThreeDVar and the 4DVar models, we approximate the posterior at the MAP as a Gaussian whose covariance is the inverse Hessian of the cost, and drop the second-derivative term of the observation misfit (the Gauss-Newton approximation). What the observations depend on decides the precision.

A single analysis time (ThreeDVar) gives

Σx∣y=(∇x2J(x⋆))−1≈(Σx−1+H(x⋆)⊤Σε−1H(x⋆))−1\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y} = \left(\nabla^2_{\vect{x}} \mathcal{J}(\vect{x}^\star)\right)^{-1} \approx \left(\gmat{\Sigma}_x^{-1} + \mat{H}(\vect{x}^\star)^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_\varepsilon^{-1} \mat{H}(\vect{x}^\star)\right)^{-1}Σx∣y​=(∇x2​J(x⋆))−1≈(Σx−1​+H(x⋆)⊤Σε−1​H(x⋆))−1
(13.1)

where J\mathcal{J}J is the 3DVar cost (5.1) and H(x⋆)∈RDy×Dx\mat{H}(\vect{x}^\star) \in \mathbb{R}^{D_y \times D_x}H(x⋆)∈RDy​×Dx​ the Jacobian of the observation operator at the MAP, so the bracket is the Gauss-Newton Hessian (5.3) evaluated at x⋆\vect{x}^\starx⋆.

In a 4DVar window (StrongFourDVar, IncrementalFourDVar, ReducedBasisFourDVar without a basis), the observations at time ttt depend on x0\vect{x}_0x0​ through the model, so the covariance is the inverse of the Gauss-Newton Hessian (8.4):

Σx∣y=Λx∣y−1\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y} = \gmat{\Lambda}_{x \mid y}^{-1}Σx∣y​=Λx∣y−1​
(13.2)

where Λx∣y∈RDx×Dx\gmat{\Lambda}_{x \mid y} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x \times D_x}Λx∣y​∈RDx​×Dx​ is that Hessian evaluated along the analysis trajectory, Ft←0\mat{F}_{t \leftarrow 0}Ft←0​ is the tangent-linear model from x0\vect{x}_0x0​ to time ttt and Ht\mat{H}_tHt​ includes the mask Mt=diag(mt)\mat{M}_t = \op{diag}(\vect{m}_t)Mt​=diag(mt​). Never-observed state components that the dynamics couple to observed ones are constrained through Ft←0\mat{F}_{t \leftarrow 0}Ft←0​, so their posterior variance drops below the prior even though no observation touches them.

For WeakFourDVar, the control is w=(x0,η1,…,ηT)∈R(T+1)Dx\vect{w} = (\vect{x}_0, \gvect{\eta}_1, \ldots, \gvect{\eta}_T) \in \mathbb{R}^{(T + 1) D_x}w=(x0​,η1​,…,ηT​)∈R(T+1)Dx​ of the weak-constraint cost (7.2), and the precision Λw∣y\gmat{\Lambda}_{w \mid y}Λw∣y​ gains Ση,t−1\gmat{\Sigma}_{\eta,t}^{-1}Ση,t−1​ on each ηt\gvect{\eta}_tηt​. The adapter returns the x0\vect{x}_0x0​ block of Λw∣y−1\gmat{\Lambda}_{w \mid y}^{-1}Λw∣y−1​, which is the Kalman-smoother covariance of x0\vect{x}_0x0​ in the linear-Gaussian case. Pass analysis=(x0, eta).

gaussx.diag reads the posterior variances off the lazy operator. The block continues the one above.

It prints 0.17 0.13 0.15 0.38 0.85 1.00. Cells 3 and 4 are never observed, but the ring average carries them into observed cells within the window, so their variance drops below the prior’s 1; cell 5 would need a third step to reach an observed cell and keeps the prior variance.

The adapter reads the model’s obs_op and rollout (an .as_analysis_step() wrapper works too) and the window’s length and mask from batch, which must hold a single window. It returns cov as an AbstractLinearOperator: each mat-vec is a Krylov solve (lineax.CG by default, solver= to change it), and each Krylov iteration is one tangent-linear plus one adjoint pass through the rollout. Nothing is materialised.

A linear control, parameterization=LinearParameterization(basis, background) or ReducedBasisFourDVar(basis=...), has x=μx+Φz\vect{x} = \gvect{\mu}_x + \gmat{\Phi} \vect{z}x=μx​+Φz and the penalty 12z⊤Σz−1z\frac{1}{2} \vect{z}^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} \vect{z}21​z⊤Σz−1​z in place of the background term (the reduced-basis cost (23.5) of chapter 23). The adapters then build the precision in coefficient space and map it to the state:

Σz∣y=(Σz−1+Φ⊤H0:T⊤Σε,0:T−1H0:TΦ)−1, and Σx∣y=ΦΣz∣yΦ⊤\gmat{\Sigma}_{z \mid y} = \left(\gmat{\Sigma}_z^{-1} + \gmat{\Phi}^\top \mat{H}_{0:T}^\top \gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,0:T}^{-1} \mat{H}_{0:T} \gmat{\Phi}\right)^{-1}, \text{ and } \gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y} = \gmat{\Phi} \gmat{\Sigma}_{z \mid y} \gmat{\Phi}^\topΣz∣y​=(Σz−1​+Φ⊤H0:T⊤​Σε,0:T−1​H0:T​Φ)−1, and Σx∣y​=ΦΣz∣y​Φ⊤
(13.3)

where Φ\gmat{\Phi}Φ is the basis, Σz\gmat{\Sigma}_zΣz​ the prior covariance of the coefficients z\vect{z}z, H0:T\mat{H}_{0:T}H0:T​ the single-time H\mat{H}H or the strong-constraint window map HtFt←0\mat{H}_t \mat{F}_{t \leftarrow 0}Ht​Ft←0​ stacked over ttt, and Σε,0:T\gmat{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon,0:T}Σε,0:T​ the block-diagonal covariance of the stacked observations. The whitened control Φ=Lx\gmat{\Phi} = \mat{L}_xΦ=Lx​, Σz=I\gmat{\Sigma}_z = \mat{I}Σz​=I with Σx=LxLx⊤\gmat{\Sigma}_x = \mat{L}_x \mat{L}_x^\topΣx​=Lx​Lx⊤​ gives exactly the state-space posterior. Any other parameterization (a NeuralFieldParameterization, say) and a parameterized WeakFourDVar raise ValueError: their prior is not Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​, and their decode is not affine.

Gaussian-likelihood only. For non-Gaussian likelihoods (heavy tails, censored, count data), the Laplace approximation is wrong; use ensemble or amortized posteriors.

Unimodal-around-MAP only. If the posterior is multimodal, the Laplace approximation captures only the local mode. Diagnose by running multiple MAPs from perturbed starting points.

3. Gauss-Newton Hessian¶

GaussNewtonHessian builds the same (13.2) as LaplaceCovariance and exposes the Krylov budget (n_krylov, tol). The block continues the one above.

It prints True.

IncrementalFourDVar assembles this operator at its last outer iterate during the analysis; the adapter rebuilds it at the analysis it is given, at the cost of one linearisation of the rollout.

With n_krylov=50, a mat-vec costs up to 50 tangent-linear plus adjoint passes, which is still much cheaper than materialising Σx∣y\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y}Σx∣y​.

4. Ensemble (filterax bridge)¶

For multimodal posteriors, non-Gaussian likelihoods, or hybrid EnVar (Epic 9), build the posterior from an ensemble of analyses:

Σx∣y≈1NE−1∑e∈Ex~(e)x~(e)⊤, with x~(e)=x(e)−xˉ\gmat{\Sigma}_{x \mid y} \approx \frac{1}{N_\mathcal{E} - 1} \sum_{e \in \mathcal{E}} \tilde{\vect{x}}^{(e)} \tilde{\vect{x}}^{(e)\top}, \text{ with } \tilde{\vect{x}}^{(e)} = \vect{x}^{(e)} - \bar{\vect{x}}Σx∣y​≈NE​−11​e∈E∑​x~(e)x~(e)⊤, with x~(e)=x(e)−xˉ
(13.4)

where each member x(e)∈RDx\vect{x}^{(e)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_x}x(e)∈RDx​ is a separate vardax analysis with a perturbed initial condition or observation realisation, NEN_\mathcal{E}NE​ is the number of members and xˉ\bar{\vect{x}}xˉ their mean.

Here the NE=8N_\mathcal{E} = 8NE​=8 members re-run the strong 4DVar above on perturbed observations y(e)=y+σε(e)\vect{y}^{(e)} = \vect{y} + \sigma \gvect{\varepsilon}^{(e)}y(e)=y+σε(e), and ζ=1.1\zeta = 1.1ζ=1.1 inflates the sample covariance (13.4). Validation gates¶

Posteriors are only as good as they agree with reality, so the six-step cycle checks a fast posterior against its oracle with the posterior-agreement gate (14.4), the Jacobian gate (14.3) and simulation-based calibration; chapter 14 states the gates and the functions that implement them.
