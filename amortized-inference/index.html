Amortized Inference

The six methods covered so far (chapters 4 to 9) all solve a fresh minimisation per event. A new observation triggers a new analysis, which is fine for retrospective work but too slow for real-time. Amortized inference learns a function qϕ(x∣y)q_\phi(x \mid y)qϕ​(x∣y) that maps observations directly to posteriors, in a single forward pass.

The cost of training amortizes over many events; the per-event cost becomes a single neural network evaluation. This is the difference between research and operations.

Formulation¶

Learn a conditional density qϕ(x∣y)q_\phi(x \mid y)qϕ​(x∣y) that approximates the exact Bayesian posterior (1.1):

ϕ∗=arg⁡min⁡ϕEy∼p(y)KL(qϕ(⋅∣y)∥p(⋅∣y))\phi^* = \underset{\phi}{\arg\min} \mathbb{E}_{y \sim p(y)} \mathrm{KL}\left(q_\phi(\cdot \mid y) \| p(\cdot \mid y)\right)ϕ∗=ϕargmin​Ey∼p(y)​KL(qϕ​(⋅∣y)∥p(⋅∣y))
(10.1)

where ϕ\phiϕ are the network parameters and the expectation runs over the observations the model will see.

When the prior and forward model are samplable (which they are when supplied by somax or plumax), the training distribution comes from simulation:

  1. Draw x∼p(x)x \sim p(x)x∼p(x) from the prior.

  2. Simulate y=H(M(x))+εy = H(M(x)) + \varepsilony=H(M(x))+ε, the observation model (2.1) applied to the forecast M(x)M(x)M(x).

  3. Train qϕq_\phiqϕ​ to recover xxx from yyy on these synthetic pairs.

This is “simulation-based inference” (Cranmer et al. 2020). The training distribution is fully controlled (every (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) pair has known ground truth), but the deployment distribution must match for qϕq_\phiqϕ​ to be calibrated.

Three head variants¶

vardax AmortizedPosterior exposes three head choices via the head_type config:

Conditional normalising flow (head_type="flow")¶

Learn an invertible map fϕf_\phifϕ​ from a base Gaussian to the posterior, conditioned on the observation context:

x=fϕ(z;cψ(y)), with z∼N(0,I)x = f_\phi(z; c_\psi(y)), \text{ with } z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)x=fϕ​(z;cψ​(y)), with z∼N(0,I)
(10.2)

where cψ(y)c_\psi(y)cψ​(y) is the context vector from an observation encoder with parameters ψ\psiψ. The change of variables gives the density

log⁡qϕ(x∣y)=log⁡pz(fϕ−1(x;cψ(y)))−log⁡∣det⁡∂fϕ/∂z∣\log q_\phi(x \mid y) = \log p_z\left(f_\phi^{-1}(x; c_\psi(y))\right) - \log \left| \det \partial f_\phi / \partial z \right|logqϕ​(x∣y)=logpz​(fϕ−1​(x;cψ​(y)))−log∣det∂fϕ​/∂z∣
(10.3)

where pzp_zpz​ is the base Gaussian density and the Jacobian is taken at z=fϕ−1(x;cψ(y))z = f_\phi^{-1}(x; c_\psi(y))z=fϕ−1​(x;cψ​(y)).

Exact density, exact samples. Use gauss_flows for the flow implementation. Good for moderate-dimensional state (up to ∼104\sim 10^4∼104). Higher-dimensional flows are open research.

Score-based diffusion (head_type="score")¶

Learn the score of a noise-perturbed posterior at noise scale ttt:

sϕ(x,t∣y)≈∇xlog⁡pt(x∣y)s_\phi(x, t \mid y) \approx \nabla_x \log p_t(x \mid y)sϕ​(x,t∣y)≈∇x​logpt​(x∣y)
(10.4)

where ptp_tpt​ is the posterior perturbed by noise up to scale ttt. Sampling runs the reverse SDE

dx=[f(x,t)−g(t)2sϕ(x,t∣y)]dt+g(t)dwˉd x = \left[f(x, t) - g(t)^2 s_\phi(x, t \mid y) \right] d t + g(t) d \bar{w}dx=[f(x,t)−g(t)2sϕ​(x,t∣y)]dt+g(t)dwˉ
(10.5)

backwards in ttt, where fff and ggg are the drift and diffusion of the forward noising process and wˉ\bar{w}wˉ is a reverse-time Wiener process.

No exact density (sampling-only). High capacity for multimodal posteriors, since the diffusion process can represent disconnected components, which flows struggle with. Scales to higher state dimensions than flows.

Direct regression (head_type="regression")¶

Predict Gaussian posterior parameters directly:

qϕ(x∣y)=N(x;μϕ(y),Σϕ(y))q_\phi(x \mid y) = \mathcal{N}\left(x; \mu_\phi(y), \Sigma_\phi(y)\right)qϕ​(x∣y)=N(x;μϕ​(y),Σϕ​(y))
(10.6)

where μϕ\mu_\phiμϕ​ and Σϕ\Sigma_\phiΣϕ​ are the network’s predicted posterior mean and covariance.

Cheapest by far. Restricted to unimodal Gaussian posteriors. Useful when the posterior is known to be Gaussian (e.g., as a learned replacement for the Laplace approximation (13.1) around an IncrementalFourDVar MAP).

Training objective¶

Flow and regression heads use maximum likelihood on simulated pairs:

LMLE(ϕ)=−E(x,y)∼psimlog⁡qϕ(x∣y)\mathcal{L}_\text{MLE}(\phi) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim p_\text{sim}} \log q_\phi(x \mid y)LMLE​(ϕ)=−E(x,y)∼psim​​logqϕ​(x∣y)
(10.7)

where psimp_\text{sim}psim​ is the joint distribution of the simulated pairs. Score heads use denoising score matching:

LDSM(ϕ)=Et,x,y,ε∥sϕ(xt,t∣y)−∇xtlog⁡pt(xt∣x)∥2\mathcal{L}_\text{DSM}(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{t, x, y, \varepsilon} \left\| s_\phi(x_t, t \mid y) - \nabla_{x_t} \log p_t(x_t \mid x) \right\|^2LDSM​(ϕ)=Et,x,y,ε​∥sϕ​(xt​,t∣y)−∇xt​​logpt​(xt​∣x)∥2
(10.8)

where xt=x+σ(t)εx_t = x + \sigma(t)\varepsilonxt​=x+σ(t)ε is the noised state, ε∼N(0,I)\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)ε∼N(0,I), and σ(t)\sigma(t)σ(t) is the noise scale at ttt.

Both reduce to a standard training step over simulated minibatches: amortized_train_step is chapter 9’s train_step with the negative log-likelihood (10.7) in place of the reconstruction loss (9.3).

The toy problem has a state of T=1T = 1T=1 time and N=4N = 4N=4 cells with prior p(x)=N(0,I)p(x) = \mathcal{N}(0, I)p(x)=N(0,I) and observations y=x+σεy = x + \sigma \varepsilony=x+σε, σ=0.5\sigma = 0.5σ=0.5, fully observed. Its exact posterior is N(y/(1+σ2),σ2/(1+σ2)I)\mathcal{N}(y / (1 + \sigma^2), \sigma^2 / (1 + \sigma^2) I)N(y/(1+σ2),σ2/(1+σ2)I), the BLUE (4.4) and (4.5) with B=IB = IB=I, H=IH = IH=I and R=σ2IR = \sigma^2 IR=σ2I, which the trained head should recover. Validation: the hard gates¶

Amortized inference is dangerous when miscalibrated. A well-trained flow with a tight qϕq_\phiqϕ​ that’s centred wrong gives confident wrong answers. Decision D12 (the six-step cycle, chapter 14) requires three gates before promoting an amortized head to operational use:

1. Posterior agreement¶

The amortized MAP must fall within 1σpost1\sigma_\text{post}1σpost​ of an oracle MAP from a slower method (StrongFourDVar / IncrementalFourDVar):

∣xamort∗−xoracle∗∣σpost, oracle≤1\frac{|x^*_\text{amort} - x^*_\text{oracle}|}{\sigma_\text{post, oracle}} \le 1σpost, oracle​∣xamort∗​−xoracle∗​∣​≤1
(10.9)

where σpost, oracle\sigma_\text{post, oracle}σpost, oracle​ is the oracle’s posterior standard deviation, cell by cell. This is the posterior gate (14.4) of chapter 14 applied to the amortized map.

For the toy problem the oracle is ThreeDVar with B=IB = IB=I and R=σ2IR = \sigma^2 IR=σ2I, whose Laplace posterior (13.1) is exact here because the problem is linear-Gaussian. assert_posterior_agreement reads only the mean of the fast posterior, so the amortized MAP goes in a Posterior without a covariance. The block continues the one above.

It prints posterior agreement passed.

2. Adjoint calibration¶

The amortized model’s gradient with respect to observations must match the physics-based gradient:

∥∂xamort∗/∂y−∂xoracle∗/∂y∥op∥∂xoracle∗/∂y∥op<0.05\frac{\|\partial x^*_\text{amort} / \partial y - \partial x^*_\text{oracle} / \partial y\|_\text{op}}{\|\partial x^*_\text{oracle} / \partial y\|_\text{op}} < 0.05∥∂xoracle∗​/∂y∥op​∥∂xamort∗​/∂y−∂xoracle∗​/∂y∥op​​<0.05
(10.10)

where ∥⋅∥op\|\cdot\|_\text{op}∥⋅∥op​ is the operator norm. This is the Jacobian gate (14.3) of chapter 14 applied to the amortized map, with yyy as the input. It is measured by random-vector probing (randomised tangent-linear tests for operator agreement). Without this gate, the amortized model may match the oracle MAP on the training distribution but extrapolate badly when the observation distribution shifts.

3. Simulation-based calibration (SBC)¶

Per Talts et al. (2018): sample x(j)x^{(j)}x(j) from the prior, simulate y(j)y^{(j)}y(j), draw qϕq_\phiqϕ​ samples conditioned on y(j)y^{(j)}y(j), compute the rank of x(j)x^{(j)}x(j) in the sample. If qϕq_\phiqϕ​ is calibrated, the ranks are uniform across the sample. Failure indicates over- or under-confident posteriors.

simulation_based_calibration takes three callables (posterior sampler, prior sampler, simulator) rather than the model, and reduces each state to its ℓ2\ell_2ℓ2​ norm before ranking it among the samples. With 19 samples per run the rank takes 20 values, grouped here into 5 bins of 4; a calibrated head gives about 20 runs per bin. The block continues the one above.

It prints [30, 21, 19, 18, 12] chi2 p = 0.075: the lowest bin is over-full, but the χ2\chi^2χ2 test does not reject uniformity at the p>0.01p > 0.01p>0.01 threshold.

All three gates ship in vardax._src.utils.validation, re-exported as assert_posterior_agreement, assert_adjoint_calibrated and simulation_based_calibration, and tests/test_six_step_validation.py tests them.

When amortized inference helps¶

RegimeAmortized helps?
Single retrospective analysisNo, solver-based is fine
Real-time alertsYes, sub-second
Many independent events (catalog reprocessing)Yes, amortise training cost
Same forward, varying observationsYes, train once, infer N times
Each event has a different forwardNo, would need to retrain
Posterior shape is multimodalYes, flow / score heads can represent it; Laplace can’t

Trade-offs vs solver-based methods¶

AspectSolver-based (chapters 4 to 9)Amortized (this chapter)
Cost per eventHigh (iterative solve)Low (single forward pass)
Training costNone / low (only FourDVarNet)High (head + encoder)
GeneralisationStrong (uses physics)Limited (training distribution)
Multimodal handlingHard (Laplace assumes unimodal)Easy with score-based heads
Adjoint correctnessExact via autodiffApproximate, calibrated by gates
Posterior structureGaussian (Laplace / GN-Hessian)Flexible (flow / score / Gaussian)
Failure modeSlow convergenceConfident wrong answer outside training distribution

The right pattern is both: use solver-based methods to produce oracle posteriors on a representative sample, train an amortized head against the oracle, validate via the gates, then deploy the head for real-time work. The six-step cycle (chapter 14) formalises this.
