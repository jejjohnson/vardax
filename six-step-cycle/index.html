Six-Step Inference Cycle

The “six-step cycle” is the research-to-operations methodology vardax is engineered around. Across all forward-model tiers (geophysical fluids, atmospheric transport, methane radiative transfer) the same sequence applies:

  1. Physics forward: somax / plumax.

  2. Classical inference: OI / 3DVar / 4DVar (slow, exact).

  3. Neural emulator: trained from Step 1 (fast surrogate).

  4. Emulator-based inference: the same vardax code (100 to 1000× faster).

  5. Amortized predictor: y\vect{y}y to posterior in one pass (sub-second).

  6. Improve: swap any block; the prior step is the oracle.

The key property is that Steps 2, 4, and 5 use the same vardax code. The forward model is swapped via the pipekit_cycle.ForwardModel protocol; the analysis class doesn’t know whether ftf_tft​ is physics or an emulator. The amortized predictor satisfies the same AnalysisStep protocol as the classical method it replaces.

This is what makes the cycle a cycle and not a rewrite.

Step 3: Train a neural emulator¶

The forward model is now the bottleneck. Train a surrogate gemug_\text{emu}gemu​ that mimics the physics forward:

gemu(x;θ)≈f(x)g_\text{emu}(\vect{x}; \gvect{\theta}) \approx f(\vect{x})gemu​(x;θ)≈f(x)
(14.1)

where gemug_\text{emu}gemu​ is the emulator with weights θ∈RDθ\gvect{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_\theta}θ∈RDθ​ and fff is the physics forward of Step 1. Two training-time gates must hold before promotion to Step 4.

Forward agreement¶

on held-out states x\vect{x}x, where tolfwd\mathrm{tol}_\text{fwd}tolfwd​ is the tolerance (typically 0.01). This is the obvious gate: does the emulator give the right answer on the forward pass?

Adjoint calibration, the hard gate¶

∥∂gemu/∂x−∂f/∂x∥op∥∂f/∂x∥op<0.05\frac{\Vert \partial g_\text{emu} / \partial \vect{x} - \partial f / \partial \vect{x} \Vert_\text{op}}{\Vert \partial f / \partial \vect{x} \Vert_\text{op}} < 0.05∥∂f/∂x∥op​∥∂gemu​/∂x−∂f/∂x∥op​​<0.05

where ∥⋅∥op\Vert \cdot \Vert_\text{op}∥⋅∥op​ is the operator norm of a Dx×DxD_x \times D_xDx​×Dx​ Jacobian, estimated by random-vector probing of the Jacobian difference. A fast forward with a wrong Jacobian gives wrong posteriors, because the gradient that drives the inner minimisation is mis-directed.

This gate is hard: vardax refuses to promote an emulator that fails adjoint calibration, even if the forward agreement is excellent. Wrong-direction gradients are silently corrosive.

Step 6: Improve, repeat¶

The cycle is a loop. Improvements at any step trigger re-validation downstream:

ChangeRe-validates
New physics in Step 1Steps 2, 3, 4, 5
New inference method in Step 2Step 4 (Step 2 is oracle)
Better emulator (Step 3)Steps 4, 5
Better amortized head (Step 5)Step 5 vs Step 2 / 4
New observation operatorSteps 2, 4, 5

The validation gates are the contract, since they are how you know an “improvement” is actually an improvement.

Why the cycle is the right framing¶

Three observations from operational DA:

  • Fast inference is dangerous when wrong. Replacing a 4DVar with a flow predictor that runs in milliseconds is appealing, until the flow’s posterior is miscalibrated and the alerting system fires on noise. The gates between steps make “fast” and “correct” both requirements, not just the former.

  • The forward model is the slow part, not the inference. When the forward is a high-resolution ocean / atmosphere / plume model, iteration count matters less than per-iteration cost. Replacing the forward with an emulator (Step 3) is the largest single speedup available.

  • Research and operations need the same code. A research scientist validates the methodology in a Jupyter notebook. An operational engineer deploys it behind a FastAPI handler. If the code paths diverge, drift between research validation and operational performance is inevitable. The cycle keeps them aligned.

Vardax codifies the cycle: the protocols are the same across steps, the gates are part of the test suite, and the same AnalysisStep instance can run in a notebook, a batch pipeline, and a streaming API.

Implementation¶

The validation utilities ship in vardax._src.utils.validation and are re-exported at the top level:

  • assert_adjoint_calibrated(fn_fast, fn_oracle, y, *, key, threshold=0.05, n_probes=10): random-vector operator norm probing

  • assert_posterior_agreement(p_fast, p_oracle, *, tolerance_sigma=1.0)

  • simulation_based_calibration(sample_posterior, sample_prior, simulate_obs, *, key, n_runs=200, n_samples=200): returns the ranks

assert_forward_agreement is not shipped yet; Step 3 computes the forward error inline.

tests/test_six_step_validation.py wires these into CI. The gates block merges that promote an emulator or amortized head past its validation level.
