22 · Neural-Field 4D-Var

Strong-constraint 4D-Var (6.1) controls the initial state and reaches every later time by rolling a model forward (1.3). SpaceTimeFieldVar drops the rollout: the whole assimilation window is one continuous field

22.1 Running example: SSH from along-track altimetry¶

Satellite altimeters sample sea-surface height (SSH) along narrow ground tracks, a few thousand scattered (ti,si,yi)(t_i, \vect{s}_i, y_i)(ti​,si​,yi​) points per day with errors σi\sigma_iσi​, and nothing in between. A gridded 4D-Var first interpolates those points onto the model grid (GridPointObs) and then needs a background covariance Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​ to spread their information. A neural field reads the tracks as they are (PointObsBatch), and the quasi-geostrophic equations, imposed at collocation points, carry the information along the flow instead of Σx\gmat{\Sigma}_xΣx​.

22.3 Usage¶

Coordinates put time first, (t,s1,s2)(t, s_1, s_2)(t,s1​,s2​), for the field, the observations and the collocation points alike (the convention of the residuals in vardax.examples):

A small SIREN is fitted to 64 noisy samples of the decaying Taylor-Green vortex ψ(t,s)=sin⁡s1sin⁡s2e−2νt\psi(t, \vect{s}) = \sin s_1 \sin s_2 e^{-2 \nu t}ψ(t,s)=sins1​sins2​e−2νt with viscosity ν=0.1\nu = 0.1ν=0.1, which solves the unforced vorticity equation exactly; the example runs on a CPU in seconds. A real analysis uses a wider and deeper network, more collocation points and a step budget in the tens of thousands.

It prints 300 steps, ‖∇𝒥‖ = 141 and relative error 0.14, cycle state (25,); the large gradient norm is the step budget running out, not convergence.

result.grad_norm reports ∥∇θJ(θ⋆)∥\Vert \nabla_{\gvect{\theta}} \mathcal{J}(\gvect{\theta}^\star) \Vert∥∇θ​J(θ⋆)∥: a first-order optimiser stopped by its step budget is not at a stationary point, which matters if you differentiate through the analysis.

A separable field (one small network per axis, combined by a low-rank product, as in Oh 2025) evaluates a whole space-time grid in one pass and is much faster for dense collocation; any single-example eqx.Module of (t,s)(t, s)(t,s) works as field.

Collocation points¶

A ResidualPenalty with a sampler draws its points once per analysis, from key, and holds them fixed while the minimiser runs. The minimiser assumes a deterministic cost: its patience test and best-iterate tracking compare costs across steps, and the implicit adjoint differentiates the stationarity condition ∇θJ(θ⋆)=0\nabla_{\gvect{\theta}} \mathcal{J}(\gvect{\theta}^\star) = 0∇θ​J(θ⋆)=0. Resampling every step (a stochastic gradient of the expected residual) would break all three, so it is left to a custom training loop; draw enough points to resolve the residual, or refit with fresh keys.

In a cycle, as_analysis_step(grid, key=key) draws the points once, at construction, and every cycle reuses them. pipekit_cycle.DACycle passes no time, so each cycle’s PointObsBatch must use window-relative times in the same window as the collocation points; the step refuses observation times (and a t0t_0t0​) outside the collocation time range.

Choosing βres\beta_\text{res}βres​¶

The observation term is a sum over NobsN_\text{obs}Nobs​ points weighted by 1/σ21 / \sigma^21/σ2, the residual term a mean over the collocation points, so βres\beta_\text{res}βres​ trades off against roughly Nobs/(2σ2)N_\text{obs} / (2 \sigma^2)Nobs​/(2σ2) and has to grow with it. The 1-D advection test uses βres=103\beta_\text{res} = 10^3βres​=103 for 100 observations with σ=0.05\sigma = 0.05σ=0.05 (Nobs/(2σ2)=2×104N_\text{obs} / (2 \sigma^2) = 2 \times 10^4Nobs​/(2σ2)=2×104).

Use the neural field when observations are scattered in space and time, when no tangent-linear/adjoint model is at hand but the equations are known, or when the window is long enough that a sequential rollout dominates the cost. Prefer strong 4D-Var when the model is trusted (the residual weight then has to grow without bound to match it) and when a forecast from the analysis must be consistent with the model step by step. The two meet in the strong-constraint variant: fit only x(t0,⋅)x(t_0, \cdot)x(t0​,⋅) as a field and roll it out, StrongFourDVar(..., parameterization=NeuralFieldParameterization(field, coords, alpha)) (chapter 6).
