SSH Reconstruction Example

Sea surface height (SSH) reconstruction from satellite altimetry is the canonical large-scale 4DVarNet benchmark (Fablet et al. 2021, 2023). The state is a 2D field; observations are along-track samples from altimetric satellites (Jason, SARAL/AltiKa, SWOT); the gaps between tracks need to be filled.

This chapter shows three approaches to the same problem: OI as the classical baseline, IncrementalFourDVar as the operational pattern, and FourDVarNet as the learned alternative. Same data, three analyses, side-by-side comparison.

Problem setup¶

State: SSH on a regular lon-lat grid, H×W=128×128H \times W = 128 \times 128H×W=128×128 typical for a regional patch. Observations: along-track samples from one or more altimeters, with along-track noise σ∼3\sigma \sim 3σ∼3 cm and gaps of ∼100\sim 100∼100 km perpendicular to track.

The arrays ssh_observed, track_mask, ssh_truth, lat, lon, altika_uncertainty and climatology_ssh come from preprocessed altimetry and are not built on this page. The state is flattened to N=YXN = Y XN=YX cells, with Y=HY = HY=H latitudes and X=WX = WX=W longitudes, so that one Batch1D serves OI and incremental 4DVar.

Approach 1: OptimalInterpolation¶

The classical baseline is OI (chapter 4) with the masked identity (2.3) as HHH, a separable Matérn prior and a diagonal RRR.

What this gives you: the standard OceanBench DUACS baseline. Smoothed through the prior length scale. Easy to interpret, easy to validate. Misses mesoscale features that are shorter than the correlation length.

Approach 2: IncrementalFourDVar with shallow-water dynamics¶

Add a somax.ShallowWaterModel as the dynamics. Multi-time observations now constrain the trajectory through the physics, so gaps get filled by dynamical propagation rather than just smoothing.

What this gives you: physically-consistent reconstruction. The incremental machinery scales to operational grids (106 to 107 cells) thanks to the CVT-preconditioned CG (8.7).

Approach 3: FourDVarNet with learned prior¶

Train a FourDVarNet (chapter 9) on (observed, truth) patches. The learned prior φθ\varphi_\thetaφθ​ of the cost (9.1) captures the SSH manifold, and the learned gradient modulator Φϕ\Phi_\phiΦϕ​ of the update (9.2) accelerates the inner iterations.

What this gives you: state-of-the-art reconstruction on the distribution the network was trained on. Better than OI in information-rich regions; comparable to incremental 4DVar without needing somax. Limitations: extrapolates poorly outside the training distribution; posterior calibration via Laplace is less defensible than the GN-Hessian-based posterior from incremental 4DVar.

The OceanBench benchmark (Le Guillou et al. 2023) provides a standard dataset for these comparisons. The expected ordering is OI < IncrementalFourDVar ≈ FourDVarNet, with FourDVarNet matching IncrementalFourDVar on in-distribution data but degrading more gracefully when the input distribution drifts.

Operational cycling¶

For continuous SSH analysis (every overpass, every few hours), wrap the chosen analysis in a pipekit_cycle.DACycle:

The analysis_step slot accepts any of the three methods, so swapping OptimalInterpolation for IncrementalFourDVar or FourDVarNet changes one slot. Nothing in the cycling, data loading, or output serialisation changes.

OptimalInterpolation and IncrementalFourDVar have no learned parameters; they need no persistence (the prior_cov_op and config are reconstructed from numerical parameters).
